# Conjecture 4: Sequential Re-Compression Forgetting Phase Transition & Causal-State Critical Dimension

**Executive Summary.** Consider a sequential Markov re-compression chain $M_0 = f_0(X_0)$, $M_{t+1} = f_t(M_t, X_{t+1})$ compressing observations of a discrete-time linear state-space system $S_{t+1} = A S_t + W_t$, $X_t = C S_t + V_t$ ($S_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$) under bounded per-step memory dimension $d_{\mathrm{mem}}$ (or bounded bit-rate $b$ bits/step), evaluated against unknown **late queries** $q_v(S_{t+\tau}) = v^\top S_{t+\tau}$ ($v \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$, $\tau \to \infty$). Let $\mathbb{R}^d = E^{u/c} \oplus E^s = E^u \oplus E^c \oplus E^s$ be the $A$-invariant Real Jordan decomposition into unstable ($|\lambda| > 1$, $\dim E^u = d_u$), marginal ($|\lambda| = 1$, $\dim E^c = d_c$), and strictly stable ($|\lambda| < 1$, $\dim E^s = d_s$) generalized eigenspaces, and let
$$r := \dim E^{u/c} = d_u + d_c, \qquad H_{\mathrm{top}}(A) := \sum_{|\lambda_i| > 1} \log_2 |\lambda_i| = \log_2 |\det A_u|,$$
counting **algebraic** multiplicity. We prove a sharp dynamical phase transition at $d_{\mathrm{mem}}^* = r$ and $b^* = H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$:
1. **Sub-critical regime ($d_{\mathrm{mem}} < r$ or $b < H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$)**: Under linear/affine, continuous, or Lipschitz+bounded-energy regularity (or finite bit-rate), worst-case excess MSE over the full-history Kalman oracle $\hat{S}_{t|t}$ diverges exponentially ($\Omega(|\lambda|^{2(t+\tau)})$ up to Jordan factors, and $\Omega(2^{\frac{2t}{d_u}(H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)-b)})$) whenever an unstable mode $|\lambda| > 1$ is dropped or rate-starved, and diverges polynomially ($\Omega((t+\tau)^{2(k-1)})$ deterministic, $\Theta(t^{2k-1})$ stochastic variance) when a size-$k$ marginal Jordan block ($|\lambda| = 1$) loses capacity. We also resolve the $|\lambda| = 1$ zero-topological-entropy trap by separating differential zooming coders ($b > H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$ stabilizes) from fixed $2^b$-state registers (which diverge as $\Omega(\sigma^2 t \cdot 2^{-2b})$).
2. **Super-critical regime ($d_{\mathrm{mem}} \ge r$ and sufficient bit-rate)**: Under full-state ($X_t = S_t$) or decoupled ($C_s = 0$) observations with $(A_{u/c}, C_{u/c})$ observable, storing $M_t = \hat{S}_{t|t}^{u/c} \in \mathbb{R}^r$ achieves **zero** $E^{u/c}$ excess error and uniform boundedness across **all** $(t, \tau) \in \mathbb{N}_0^2$, decaying exponentially as $O(\bar{\rho}^{2\tau})$ ($\bar{\rho} \in (\rho(A_s), 1)$) in $\tau$ (**zero-decay continuity phase**). For coupled partial observations ($C_s \ne 0$), an $r$-dimensional Luenberger observer maintains $O(1)$ tracking error as $t \to \infty$ at every fixed $\tau$, while open-loop $\tau \to \infty$ equivalence to the $d$-dimensional Kalman filter $\hat{S}_{t|t}$ requires the coupled Kalman state.
3. **Discrete SDPI & Causal States**: We give a self-contained proof of the Cohen–Kempermann–Zbăganu inequality $\eta_{\mathrm{KL}}(K) \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K)$ via centered hockey-stick divergences, deduce multi-step SDPI exponential forgetting $I(S_0; M_t) \le (\prod_{s=1}^t \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K_s)) I(S_0; M_0)$, and prove that as $\tau \to \infty$, Crutchfield's causal-state manifold collapses from $\mathbb{R}^d$ to $E^{u/c}$ via $I(S_t^s; S_{t+\tau}^s) = O(\bar{\rho}^{2\tau}) \to 0$.
4. **Stress-Tests**: We rigorously analyze size-$k$ Jordan blocks at $|\lambda| = 1$ (including when the compressor retains the top coordinate $x_{t,k-1}$ and drops $x_{t,0}$), non-normal transient amplification via the Kreiss Matrix Theorem, periodic external log retrieval every $T$ steps (proving $d_{\mathrm{mem}}^* = r$ invariance for $\tau \to \infty$, $T$-dependent prefactor rescaling $\Theta(|\lambda|^{2T})$ / $\Theta(T^{2k-1})$ under full-state $\tau = 0$ resets, and $b_{\mathrm{eff}} = b + B_{\mathrm{ext}}/T$), and nonlinear/heavy-tailed edge cases.

This project forms Conjecture 4 of the **Late Query Frontier** suite (`WaveField`, `CompInflation`, `CausalPremium`, `MemoryPhase`). Section 6 states the exact scope boundary between the analytic proofs here and the machine-checked Lean 4 kernel formalization in `MemoryPhase.lean`.

## 1. System Model, Invariant Subspace Decomposition & Excess Risk

### 1.1 State-Space Dynamics and Memory Models

Consider the discrete-time linear state-space system on $\mathbb{R}^d$ ($t \in \mathbb{N}_0 = \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$):
$$S_{t+1} = A S_t + W_t, \qquad X_t = C S_t + V_t, \tag{1.1}$$
where $A \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$, $C \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d}$, $S_0 \in \mathbb{R}^d$ has mean $\mu_0$ and covariance $\Sigma_0 \succeq 0$, and $\{W_t\}_{t \ge 0} \subset \mathbb{R}^d$, $\{V_t\}_{t \ge 0} \subset \mathbb{R}^m$ are zero-mean mutually independent noise sequences, independent of $S_0$, with covariances $\Sigma_W := \mathbb{E}[W_t W_t^\top] \succeq 0$ and $\Sigma_V := \mathbb{E}[V_t V_t^\top] \succeq 0$. (In full-state observation, $C = I_d$ and $V_t \equiv 0$, so $X_t = S_t$.)

A causal **Markov re-compression chain** produces summary states $\{M_t\}_{t \ge 0}$ via
$$M_0 = f_0(X_0) \in \mathcal{M}_0, \qquad M_{t+1} = f_t(M_t, X_{t+1}) \in \mathcal{M}_{t+1}, \tag{1.2}$$
where any auxiliary encoder randomness is independent of future noise $(W_{t:\infty}, V_{t+1:\infty})$. We distinguish three capacity constraints:
1. **Dimensional constraint ($d_{\mathrm{mem}}$)**: $\mathcal{M}_t \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ under explicit regularity (Section 1.4).
2. **Causal differential bit-rate ($b$ bits/step)**: At each step $t$, an encoder transmits a discrete message $Q_t \in \mathcal{Q}_t$ with $|\mathcal{Q}_t| \le 2^b$ to a decoder that maintains $M_t = (Q_0, \dots, Q_t)$ (equivalently, a causal real-valued state estimate $\hat{S}_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ updated from $Q_t$).
3. **Fixed finite memory register ($|\mathcal{M}_t| \le 2^b$)**: The entire memory state $M_t$ itself is restricted to at most $2^b$ distinct values at every step $t$, with no unbounded external real-valued accumulator.

At time $t$, an unknown **late query** direction $v \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1} := \{v \in \mathbb{R}^d : \|v\|_2 = 1\}$ and horizon $\tau \in \mathbb{N}_0$ are revealed, requesting a measurable readout $\hat{y}_{t,\tau}(v) = g_{v,t,\tau}(M_t)$ minimizing MSE against $v^\top S_{t+\tau}$.

### 1.2 Real Jordan Canonical Form & Complex-Conjugate Block Norm Identity

By the **Real Jordan Canonical Form**, there exists an invertible matrix $P = [P_{u/c}, P_s] \in \mathrm{GL}_d(\mathbb{R})$ such that
$$P^{-1} A P = \begin{pmatrix} A_{u/c} & 0 \\ 0 & A_s \end{pmatrix}, \qquad A_{u/c} = \begin{pmatrix} A_u & 0 \\ 0 & A_c \end{pmatrix}, \tag{1.3}$$
inducing an $A$-invariant direct-sum decomposition
$$\mathbb{R}^d = E^{u/c} \oplus E^s = E^u \oplus E^c \oplus E^s,$$
where:
- $E^u$ ($d_u := \dim E^u$) is the sum of generalized eigenspaces of eigenvalues $\lambda \in \sigma(A)$ with $|\lambda| > 1$;
- $E^c$ ($d_c := \dim E^c$) is the sum of generalized eigenspaces with $|\lambda| = 1$;
- $E^{u/c} := E^u \oplus E^c$ has dimension $r := d_u + d_c$, equal to the sum of **algebraic multiplicities** of all eigenvalues with $|\lambda| \ge 1$;
- $E^s$ ($d_s := d - r$) is the sum of generalized eigenspaces with $|\lambda| < 1$, so $\rho(A_s) := \max_{\lambda \in \sigma(A_s)} |\lambda| < 1$.

Write $P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} \Pi_{u/c} \\ \Pi_s \end{pmatrix}$ with $\Pi_{u/c} \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d}$, $\Pi_s \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s \times d}$, and further split $\Pi_{u/c} = \begin{pmatrix} \Pi_u \\ \Pi_c \end{pmatrix}$, $P_{u/c} = [P_u, P_c]$. By $P^{-1} P = I_d$, we have $\Pi_{u/c} P_{u/c} = I_r$, $\Pi_s P_s = I_{d_s}$, $\Pi_{u/c} P_s = 0$, $\Pi_s P_{u/c} = 0$, and $P_{u/c} \Pi_{u/c} + P_s \Pi_s = I_d$. Setting $S_t^{u/c} := \Pi_{u/c} S_t$, $S_t^s := \Pi_s S_t$, $W_t^{u/c} := \Pi_{u/c} W_t$, and $W_t^s := \Pi_s W_t$, the dynamics decouple exactly:
$$S_{t+1}^{u/c} = A_{u/c} S_t^{u/c} + W_t^{u/c}, \qquad S_{t+1}^s = A_s S_t^s + W_t^s. \tag{1.4}$$

Each block of $P^{-1} A P$ is either a real $k \times k$ Jordan block $J_k(\lambda) = \lambda I_k + N_k$ ($\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$, where $(N_k)_{i,j} = \delta_{j, i+1}$) or a $2k \times 2k$ real Jordan block $J_{2k}^{\mathbb{R}}(|\lambda|, \theta)$ for a conjugate pair $|\lambda| e^{\pm i\theta}$ ($\theta \in (0, \pi)$).

**Lemma 1.1 (Exact Powers of Real & Complex-Conjugate Jordan Blocks).**
*(a) For any scalar $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}$, block size $k \ge 1$, $t \ge 0$, and vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^k$ (0-indexed):*
$$\big(J_k(\lambda)^t x\big)_i = \sum_{j=0}^{\min(t,\, k-1-i)} \binom{t}{j} \lambda^{t-j} x_{i+j}, \qquad 0 \le i \le k-1. \tag{1.5}$$
*(b) For a $2k \times 2k$ real Jordan block $J_{2k}^{\mathbb{R}}(|\lambda|, \theta) := I_k \otimes R(|\lambda|, \theta) + N_k \otimes I_2$, where $R(|\lambda|, \theta) := |\lambda| \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}$,*
$$\big(J_{2k}^{\mathbb{R}}(|\lambda|, \theta)^t\big)_{0\text{-th } 2\times 2\text{ block}} (u_0, \dots, u_{k-1})^\top = \sum_{j=0}^{\min(t,\, k-1)} \binom{t}{j} |\lambda|^{t-j} R\big(1, (t-j)\theta\big) u_j \quad (u_j \in \mathbb{R}^2). \tag{1.6}$$
*In particular, if $u_0 = \cdots = u_{k-2} = 0$, the top $2$-vector has exact Euclidean norm $\binom{t}{k-1} |\lambda|^{t-k+1} \|u_{k-1}\|_2$ for all $t \ge k-1$ (oscillation from $\theta$ never cancels the amplitude).*

*Proof.* Since $\lambda I_k$ and $N_k$ commute and $N_k^k = 0$, the binomial theorem gives $J_k(\lambda)^t = \sum_{j=0}^{\min(t, k-1)} \binom{t}{j} \lambda^{t-j} N_k^j$; applying $(N_k^j x)_i = x_{i+j}$ for $i + j < k$ and $0$ otherwise yields (1.5). For (b), $I_k \otimes R(|\lambda|, \theta)$ and $N_k \otimes I_2$ commute because $(I_k \otimes R)(N_k \otimes I_2) = N_k \otimes R = (N_k \otimes I_2)(I_k \otimes R)$. Using $R(|\lambda|, \theta)^{t-j} = |\lambda|^{t-j} R(1, (t-j)\theta)$ and $R(1, \phi) \in \mathrm{SO}(2)$ (orthogonal, $\|R(1,\phi) u\|_2 = \|u\|_2$) yields (1.6) and the exact norm identity. $\square$

### 1.3 Orthogonal Pythagorean Decomposition of Excess MSE

Let $\hat{S}_{t|t} := \mathbb{E}[S_t \mid X_{0:t}]$ be the full-history MMSE estimator (the exact Kalman filter when $(S_0, W_{0:t-1}, V_{0:t})$ are jointly Gaussian). Note that $S_{t+\tau} = A^\tau S_t + \Delta_{t,\tau}$ where $\Delta_{t,\tau} := \sum_{j=0}^{\tau-1} A^{\tau-1-j} W_{t+j}$. Because $(W_{t}, \dots, W_{t+\tau-1})$ are zero-mean and independent of $(X_{0:t}, M_t)$, we have $\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{t,\tau} \mid X_{0:t}, M_t] = 0$, so the full-history Bayes predictor of $v^\top S_{t+\tau}$ is
$$\hat{y}^{\mathrm{Bayes}}_{t,\tau}(v) := \mathbb{E}[v^\top S_{t+\tau} \mid X_{0:t}] = v^\top A^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t}.$$

**Proposition 1.2 (Pythagorean Excess MSE Decomposition).** *Assume $\mathbb{E}[\|S_t\|_2^2] < \infty$ and $\mathbb{E}[g_{v,t,\tau}(M_t)^2] < \infty$. If $M_t$ is $\sigma(X_{0:t})$-measurable (or uses independent auxiliary randomness independent of $(S_t, X_{0:t}, W_{t:\infty})$), then the excess MSE over $\hat{y}^{\mathrm{Bayes}}_{t,\tau}(v)$ satisfies*
$$\mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) := \mathbb{E}\big[(g_{v,t,\tau}(M_t) - v^\top S_{t+\tau})^2\big] - \mathbb{E}\big[(v^\top A^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t} - v^\top S_{t+\tau})^2\big] = \mathbb{E}\big[(g_{v,t,\tau}(M_t) - v^\top A^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t})^2\big]. \tag{1.7}$$
*Minimizing over all measurable readouts $g_{v,t,\tau}$ gives $g^*_{v,t,\tau}(M_t) = v^\top A^\tau \mathbb{E}[\hat{S}_{t|t} \mid M_t]$ and*
$$\inf_{g_{v,t,\tau}} \mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) = v^\top A^\tau \, \bar{\Sigma}_{t|M_t}^{\mathrm{Kal}} \, (A^\tau)^\top v, \qquad \bar{\Sigma}_{t|M_t}^{\mathrm{Kal}} := \mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\hat{S}_{t|t} - \mathbb{E}[\hat{S}_{t|t} \mid M_t]\big)\big(\hat{S}_{t|t} - \mathbb{E}[\hat{S}_{t|t} \mid M_t]\big)^\top\Big]. \tag{1.8}$$
*In the full-state observation regime ($X_t = S_t$), $\hat{S}_{t|t} = S_t$ and $\bar{\Sigma}_{t|M_t}^{\mathrm{Kal}} = \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Cov}(S_t \mid M_t)]$.*

*Proof.* Write $v^\top S_{t+\tau} - g_{v,t,\tau}(M_t) = \big(v^\top S_{t+\tau} - v^\top A^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t}\big) + \big(v^\top A^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t} - g_{v,t,\tau}(M_t)\big)$. Since $M_t$ is measurable with respect to $\sigma(X_{0:t}, U_t)$ where $U_t \perp (S_t, X_{0:t}, \Delta_{t,\tau})$, we have
$$\mathbb{E}\big[v^\top S_{t+\tau} - v^\top A^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t} \mid X_{0:t}, U_t\big] = v^\top A^\tau\big(\mathbb{E}[S_t \mid X_{0:t}] - \hat{S}_{t|t}\big) + v^\top \mathbb{E}[\Delta_{t,\tau} \mid X_{0:t}, U_t] = 0.$$
Because $v^\top A^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t} - g_{v,t,\tau}(M_t)$ is $\sigma(X_{0:t}, U_t)$-measurable, the law of total expectation makes the cross term vanish:
$$\mathbb{E}\big[(v^\top S_{t+\tau} - v^\top A^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t})(v^\top A^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t} - g_{v,t,\tau}(M_t))\big] = 0,$$
giving (1.7). By orthogonality of conditional expectation in $L^2$, $\mathbb{E}[\hat{S}_{t|t} \mid M_t]$ minimizes $\mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v)$ and yields (1.8). $\square$

### 1.4 Regularity Assumptions and Observability

1. **Why Regularity Is Necessary for $d_{\mathrm{mem}}$**: Set-theoretically, $\mathbb{R}^r$ and $\mathbb{R}^1$ have the same cardinality and admit Borel measurable bijections (e.g., via binary digit interleaving). Without regularity on the compression map or finite bit-rate, $d_{\mathrm{mem}} = 1$ could encode $\mathbb{R}^r$ without loss. Thus the dimensional threshold $d_{\mathrm{mem}}^* = r$ is established under any of the standard regularities:
   - **(R-Lin)** Linear/affine compression $M_{t+1} = F_t M_t + G_t X_{t+1} + b_t$ (or arbitrary Markov map $M_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ paired with an affine readout $g_{v,t,\tau}(M_t) = h_{v,t,\tau}^\top M_t + c_{v,t,\tau}$);
   - **(R-Cont)** Continuous encoding $S_0^{u/c} \mapsto M_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ on an open subset $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^r$ (for fixed noise realization);
   - **(R-Lip)** Bounded-energy memory $M_t \in [-B, B]^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ with an $L$-Lipschitz decoder $M_t \mapsto \hat{S}_t(M_t) \in \mathbb{R}^r$.
2. **Observability of $(A_{u/c}, C_{u/c})$**: Write $C P = [C_{u/c}, C_s]$ with $C_{u/c} := C P_{u/c} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times r}$ and $C_s := C P_s \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_s}$. Throughout partial-observation statements, we assume **$(A_{u/c}, C_{u/c})$ is observable** (equivalently detectable, since every eigenvalue of $A_{u/c}$ has $|\lambda| \ge 1$). Otherwise, an unobservable unstable mode cannot be tracked even by the full-history Kalman filter $\hat{S}_{t|t}$, causing total state MSE to diverge for all $d_{\mathrm{mem}}$ while masking the gap in excess MSE over $\hat{S}_{t|t}$.

## 2. Sub-Critical Regime ($d_{\mathrm{mem}} < r$ or $b < H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$)

### 2.1 Dimensional Sub-Criticality ($d_{\mathrm{mem}} < r$)

If $d_{\mathrm{mem}} < d_u$, then by rank-nullity any $d_{\mathrm{mem}}$-dimensional linear summary necessarily drops at least one unstable direction in $E^u$. If $d_u \le d_{\mathrm{mem}} < r = d_u + d_c$, there is a sharp dichotomy: *either* an unstable mode in $E^u$ is unretained (meaning $\ker(\Sigma_{M_t, \hat{S}^u}) \ne \{0\}$ under linear/affine readouts, or that on an open set in $E^u$ with $E^c$-coordinates fixed the encoder factors through a $< d_u$-dimensional map), producing exponential blow-up (Theorem 2.1); *or* all $d_u$ directions of $E^u$ are retained, leaving at most $d_{\mathrm{mem}} - d_u < d_c$ dimensions for $E^c$ and forcing at least one marginal direction in $E^c$ to be dropped, producing polynomial blow-up (Theorem 2.2).

**Theorem 2.1 (Exponential Divergence for Unretained $|\lambda| > 1$ Modes).**
*Suppose either $d_{\mathrm{mem}} < d_u$, or $d_u \le d_{\mathrm{mem}} < r$ with an unstable mode in $E^u$ unretained (specifically, $\ker(\Sigma_{M_t, \hat{S}^u}) \ne \{0\}$ in Part 1, or $M_t$ factoring through $\mathbb{R}^{d'}$ with $d' < d_u$ on $E^u$ in Parts 2–3), and $\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{S}_{t|t}^u) \succeq c_t I_{d_u}$ with $c_t > 0$.*
1. **(R-Lin) Affine readout / linear compression**: *For any random vector $M_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ (with finite second moments) and any family of affine readouts $g_{v,t,\tau}(M_t) = h_{v,t,\tau}^\top M_t + c_{v,t,\tau}$,*
   $$\sup_{v \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}} \inf_{h_{v,t,\tau}, c_{v,t,\tau}} \mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) \ge \frac{\lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{S}_{t|t}^u))}{\|\Pi_u\|_2^2} \, \sigma_{\min}(A_u^\tau)^2, \tag{2.1}$$
   *where $\sigma_{\min}(A_u^\tau) = \|A_u^{-\tau}\|_2^{-1} = \Omega\big(|\lambda_{\min}(A_u)|^\tau (1+\tau)^{-(k_{\max}-1)}\big)$ and $k_{\max}$ is the largest Jordan block size in $A_u$. Furthermore, if $\Sigma_0 \succeq \sigma_0^2 I_d$ (for all $t \ge 0$) or $\Sigma_W \succeq \sigma_W^2 I_d$ (for all $t \ge 1$) under full-state observation, or observable $(A_{u/c}, C_{u/c})$ with bounded Kalman error $\sup_t \mathbb{E}[\|S_t^u - \hat{S}_{t|t}^u\|_2^2] < \infty$, then $\lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{S}_{t|t}^u)) = \Omega\big(|\lambda_{\min}(A_u)|^{2t} (1+t)^{-2(k_{\max}-1)}\big)$, yielding*
   $$\sup_{v \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}} \mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) = \Omega\big(|\lambda_{\min}(A_u)|^{2(t+\tau)} (1+t)^{-2(k_{\max}-1)} (1+\tau)^{-2(k_{\max}-1)}\big)$$
   *(and clean $\Omega(|\lambda|^{2(t+\tau)})$ whenever the unretained unstable mode $|\lambda| > 1$ lies in a $1 \times 1$ or diagonalizable Jordan block).*
2. **(R-Cont) Continuous encoding**: *In the full-state regime $X_t = S_t$ with $W_t = 0$ and initial state $S_0^u$ in a nonempty open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ containing a closed ball $\overline{B_R^{d_u}(s_*)} \subseteq U$ ($R > 0$ independent of $t$), any continuous encoder $\Phi_t : U \to \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ with $d_{\mathrm{mem}} < d_u$ (or factoring continuously through $\mathbb{R}^{d'}$ with $d' < d_u$ on $U$) admits an antipodal collision pair $s_{1,t}, s_{2,t} \in U$ with $\Phi_t(s_{1,t}) = \Phi_t(s_{2,t})$ and $t$-uniform separation $\|s_{1,t} - s_{2,t}\|_2 = 2R > 0$, forcing $\sup_{v \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1},\, s_0^u \in \{s_{1,t}, s_{2,t}\}} \mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) = \Omega\big(|\lambda_{\min}(A_u)|^{2(t+\tau)}(1+t+\tau)^{-2(k_{\max}-1)}\big)$ uniformly in $(t,\tau)$ for any measurable decoder.*
3. **(R-Lip) Lipschitz bounded-energy decoding**: *If $M_t \in [-B, B]^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ and the state estimator $\Psi_t : [-B, B]^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}} \to \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ is $L$-Lipschitz, while $S_t^u$ covers a ball $B_{R_t}^{d_u}(0)$ with $R_t = \sigma_{\min}(A_u^t) R_0 = \Omega\big(|\lambda_{\min}(A_u)|^t (1+t)^{-(k_{\max}-1)}\big)$, then the worst-case $L^2$ error at time $t$ satisfies $\sup_{\|s_0^u\|\le R_0} \|s_t^u - \Psi_t(M_t)\|_2 \ge \min\big(R_t/2,\, c (L B)^{-d_{\mathrm{mem}}/(d_u - d_{\mathrm{mem}})} R_t^{d_u/(d_u - d_{\mathrm{mem}})}\big) = \Omega(R_t)$ (with $d_{\mathrm{mem}}$ replaced by $d'$ if factoring through $\mathbb{R}^{d'}$, $d' < d_u$).*

*Proof.*
*(1)* Let $\Sigma_{\hat{S}^u, M_t} := \mathrm{Cov}(\hat{S}_{t|t}^u, M_t) \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u \times d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ and $\Sigma_{M_t, \hat{S}^u} := \Sigma_{\hat{S}^u, M_t}^\top$. Whenever $d_{\mathrm{mem}} < d_u$, $\mathrm{rank}(\Sigma_{M_t, \hat{S}^u}) \le d_{\mathrm{mem}} < d_u$, so by rank-nullity $\ker(\Sigma_{M_t, \hat{S}^u}) \ne \{0\}$; under the hypothesis of Part 1, $\ker(\Sigma_{M_t, \hat{S}^u}) \ne \{0\}$ holds directly even when $d_u \le d_{\mathrm{mem}} < r$. Hence there exists a unit vector $u_1 \in \ker(\Sigma_{M_t, \hat{S}^u}) \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ ($\|u_1\|_2 = 1$). Since all eigenvalues of $A_u$ satisfy $|\lambda| > 1$, $A_u$ is invertible. Define
$$w := \frac{(A_u^{-\top})^\tau u_1}{\|(A_u^{-\top})^\tau u_1\|_2} \in \mathbb{S}^{d_u - 1}, \qquad v := \frac{\Pi_u^\top w}{\|\Pi_u^\top w\|_2} \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}.$$
Because $\Pi_u P_u = I_{d_u}$, $\Pi_u P_c = 0$, $\Pi_u P_s = 0$, we have $v^\top P_{u} = w^\top / \|\Pi_u^\top w\|_2$ and $v^\top P_c = 0$, $v^\top P_s = 0$, whence
$$v^\top A^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t} = \frac{w^\top A_u^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t}^u}{\|\Pi_u^\top w\|_2} = \frac{u_1^\top \hat{S}_{t|t}^u}{\|\Pi_u^\top w\|_2 \, \|(A_u^{-\top})^\tau u_1\|_2}.$$
Because $u_1^\top \Sigma_{\hat{S}^u, M_t} = 0$, the random scalar $u_1^\top \hat{S}_{t|t}^u$ is uncorrelated with every coordinate of $M_t$, so the best affine approximation of $u_1^\top \hat{S}_{t|t}^u$ from $M_t$ is the constant $\mathbb{E}[u_1^\top \hat{S}_{t|t}^u]$, leaving MSE equal to $\mathrm{Var}(u_1^\top \hat{S}_{t|t}^u) \ge \lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{S}_{t|t}^u))$. Using $\|\Pi_u^\top w\|_2 \le \|\Pi_u\|_2$ and $\|(A_u^{-\top})^\tau u_1\|_2 \le \|A_u^{-\tau}\|_2 = \sigma_{\min}(A_u^\tau)^{-1}$ proves (2.1). Since $\rho(A_u^{-1}) = |\lambda_{\min}(A_u)|^{-1} < 1$, Lemma 1.1 applied to the Jordan blocks of $A_u^{-1}$ gives $\|A_u^{-\tau}\|_2 \le C (1+\tau)^{k_{\max}-1} |\lambda_{\min}(A_u)|^{-\tau}$, yielding the $\tau$-growth bound. For $t$-growth, note that $\Pi_u P_u = I_{d_u}$ implies $\Pi_u \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u \times d}$ has full row rank $d_u$ ($\sigma_{\min}(\Pi_u) > 0$). Thus $\Sigma_0 \succeq \sigma_0^2 I_d$ implies $\Sigma_0^u = \Pi_u \Sigma_0 \Pi_u^\top \succeq \sigma_0^2 \sigma_{\min}(\Pi_u)^2 I_{d_u}$, and conjugating by $A_u^t$ yields
$$\mathrm{Cov}(S_t^u) = A_u^t \Sigma_0^u (A_u^\top)^t + \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} A_u^k \Sigma_W^u (A_u^\top)^k \succeq A_u^t \Sigma_0^u (A_u^\top)^t \succeq \sigma_0^2 \sigma_{\min}(\Pi_u)^2 \sigma_{\min}(A_u^t)^2 I_{d_u}.$$
Similarly, $\Sigma_W \succeq \sigma_W^2 I_d$ implies $\Sigma_W^u \succeq \sigma_W^2 \sigma_{\min}(\Pi_u)^2 I_{d_u}$, so for all $t \ge 1$ the $k = t-1$ summand gives $\mathrm{Cov}(S_t^u) \succeq A_u^{t-1} \Sigma_W^u (A_u^\top)^{t-1} \succeq \frac{\sigma_W^2 \sigma_{\min}(\Pi_u)^2}{\|A_u\|_2^2} \sigma_{\min}(A_u^t)^2 I_{d_u}$. In either case, $\lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Cov}(S_t^u)) \ge c_0 \sigma_{\min}(A_u^t)^2$ for a constant $c_0 > 0$. Let $C_{\mathrm{Kal}}^u := \sup_{t \ge 0} \mathbb{E}[\|S_t^u - \hat{S}_{t|t}^u\|_2^2] < \infty$. By the $L^2$ triangle inequality,
$$\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(u_1^\top \hat{S}_{t|t}^u)} \ge \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(u_1^\top S_t^u)} - \sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(u_1^\top (S_t^u - \hat{S}_{t|t}^u))} \ge \sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Cov}(S_t^u))} - \sqrt{C_{\mathrm{Kal}}^u}.$$
Since $\sigma_{\min}(A_u^t) = \Omega(|\lambda_{\min}(A_u)|^t (1+t)^{-(k_{\max}-1)}) \to \infty$, for all sufficiently large $t$ we obtain $\lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{S}_{t|t}^u)) \ge \frac{1}{4}\lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Cov}(S_t^u)) = \Omega(\sigma_{\min}(A_u^t)^2)$. (In the special case where a compressor drops a specific diagonalizable mode $\lambda$ with $|\lambda| > 1$, the lower bound reduces to the scalar recursion $e_{t+1} = \lambda e_t + w_t$ with $\Omega(|\lambda|^{2(t+\tau)})$.)

*(2)* Since $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ is nonempty and open, pick $s_* \in U$ and $R > 0$ (independent of $t$) such that the closed ball $\overline{B_R^{d_u}(s_*)} \subseteq U$. Set $d' := d_{\mathrm{mem}}$ when $d_{\mathrm{mem}} < d_u$, or let $\Phi_t = \psi_t \circ \tilde{\Phi}_t$ factor through a continuous map $\tilde{\Phi}_t : U \to \mathbb{R}^{d'}$ with $d' < d_u$ when $d_u \le d_{\mathrm{mem}} < r$. Define the continuous map $f_t : \mathbb{S}^{d_u - 1} \to \mathbb{R}^{d'}$ by $f_t(u) := \tilde{\Phi}_t(s_* + R u)$ (taking $\tilde{\Phi}_t = \Phi_t$ when $d_{\mathrm{mem}} < d_u$). Because $d' \le d_u - 1$, the **Borsuk–Ulam theorem** yields an antipodal unit vector $u_t \in \mathbb{S}^{d_u - 1}$ such that $f_t(u_t) = f_t(-u_t)$. Setting $s_{1,t} := s_* + R u_t \in U$ and $s_{2,t} := s_* - R u_t \in U$, we obtain $\Phi_t(s_{1,t}) = \Phi_t(s_{2,t})$ with $t$-uniform separation $\|s_{1,t} - s_{2,t}\|_2 = 2R > 0$. Fixing the remaining initial coordinates $(S_0^c, S_0^s) = (s_*^c, s_*^s)$ and setting $W_t = 0$, any decoder $g_{v,t,\tau}$ outputs the identical prediction $y = g_{v,t,\tau}(\Phi_t(s_{1,t})) = g_{v,t,\tau}(\Phi_t(s_{2,t}))$ for both $s_{1,t}$ and $s_{2,t}$. Choosing
$$w_t := \frac{A_u^{t+\tau}(s_{1,t} - s_{2,t})}{\|A_u^{t+\tau}(s_{1,t} - s_{2,t})\|_2} \in \mathbb{S}^{d_u - 1}, \qquad v_t := \frac{\Pi_u^\top w_t}{\|\Pi_u^\top w_t\|_2} \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1},$$
we have $|v_t^\top A^{t+\tau} P_u(s_{1,t} - s_{2,t})| = \|A_u^{t+\tau}(s_{1,t} - s_{2,t})\|_2 / \|\Pi_u^\top w_t\|_2 \ge \frac{2R}{\|\Pi_u\|_2} \sigma_{\min}(A_u^{t+\tau})$. By the triangle inequality, at least one of $s_0^u \in \{s_{1,t}, s_{2,t}\}$ incurs error $\ge \frac{R}{\|\Pi_u\|_2} \sigma_{\min}(A_u^{t+\tau})$, so
$$\sup_{v \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1},\, s_0^u \in \{s_{1,t}, s_{2,t}\}} \mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) \ge \frac{R^2}{\|\Pi_u\|_2^2} \sigma_{\min}(A_u^{t+\tau})^2 = \Omega\big(|\lambda_{\min}(A_u)|^{2(t+\tau)}(1+t+\tau)^{-2(k_{\max}-1)}\big)$$
uniformly in $(t, \tau)$.

*(3)* Suppose $\sup_{s_0^u \in B_{R_0}^{d_u}} \|s_t^u - \Psi_t(M_t)\|_2 \le \varepsilon < R_t/2$. Any $\varepsilon$-net of $[-B, B]^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ in $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ of step $\varepsilon / (L \sqrt{d_{\mathrm{mem}}})$ has cardinality at most $N_{\mathrm{cov}} \le \big(\lceil \frac{2 B L \sqrt{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}}{\varepsilon} \rceil\big)^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$, so its $L$-Lipschitz image covers $\Psi_t([-B, B]^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}})$ by $N_{\mathrm{cov}}$ Euclidean balls of radius $\varepsilon$, and thus covers $B_{R_t}^{d_u}(0)$ by balls of radius $2\varepsilon$. Comparing Euclidean volumes in $\mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ gives $(R_t / (2\varepsilon))^{d_u} \le N_{\mathrm{cov}} \le (C_1 L B / \varepsilon)^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ (for $\varepsilon \le C_1 L B$), i.e., $\varepsilon^{d_u - d_{\mathrm{mem}}} \ge (2^{-d_u} (C_1 L B)^{-d_{\mathrm{mem}}}) R_t^{d_u}$. Since $R_t = \sigma_{\min}(A_u^t) R_0 = \Omega\big(|\lambda_{\min}(A_u)|^t (1+t)^{-(k_{\max}-1)}\big) \to \infty$, for large $t$ the bound forces $\varepsilon \ge R_t / 2$ (and likewise with $d_{\mathrm{mem}}$ replaced by $d' < d_u$ if factoring through $[-B,B]^{d'}$). $\square$

Next, suppose $d_u \le d_{\mathrm{mem}} < r = d_u + d_c$ and all of $E^u$ is retained, so at least one marginal Jordan block $J_k(\lambda)$ ($|\lambda| = 1$, $k \ge 1$) loses at least one dimension of capacity. We check **both** possible failure geometries (dropping the top mode $e_{k-1}$ vs. keeping $e_{k-1}$ and dropping $e_0$):

**Theorem 2.2 (Marginal $|\lambda| = 1$ & Jordan-Block Polynomial Divergence).**
*Consider a size-$k$ real Jordan block $J_k(\lambda)$ with $|\lambda| = 1$ (or $2k \times 2k$ complex block $J_{2k}^{\mathbb{R}}(1, \theta)$) in $E^c$, under full-state observation ($X_t = S_t$).*
1. **Deterministic perturbation ($\Sigma_W = 0$)**: *If the compressor drops the top Jordan direction $e_{k-1}$ (retaining at most $k-1$ coordinates orthogonal to $e_{k-1}$, e.g. retaining only the eigenvector $e_0$), an initial uncertainty $\delta e_{k-1}$ yields error at coordinate $0$ at horizon $t + \tau \ge k - 1$:*
   $$\big|\big(J_k(\lambda)^{t+\tau}(\delta e_{k-1})\big)_0\big| = |\delta| \binom{t+\tau}{k-1} \ge \frac{|\delta|}{(k-1)!} (t + \tau + 1 - k)^{k-1} = \Omega\big((t+\tau)^{k-1}\big),$$
   *so $\sup_{v \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}} \mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) = \Omega\big((t+\tau)^{2(k-1)}\big)$.*
2. **Stochastic i.i.d. noise ($\Sigma_W \succeq \sigma_W^2 I_k$, $\sigma_W > 0$)**: *Regardless of which $(k-1)$-dimensional linear functional $M_t = H_t^\top S_t$ ($H_t \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times (k-1)}$) is stored in $M_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ (under linear/affine readouts, or arbitrary measurable readouts when $W_t$ is Gaussian):*
   - *At $\tau = 0$, if $M_t$ retains $(x_{t,1}, \dots, x_{t,k-1})$ and drops $x_{t,0}$, the linear-MMSE (Schur-complement) / Gaussian conditional variance of $x_{t,0}$ STILL diverges at the full $\Theta(\sigma_W^2 t^{2k-1})$ rate! Specifically, for $k = 2$, $\Sigma_W = \sigma_W^2 I_2$, and $S_0 = 0$, letting $\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{lin}}(x_{t,0} \mid x_{t,1})$ denote the linear-MMSE variance (equal to $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{Var}(x_{t,0} \mid x_{t,1})]$ under Gaussian noise):*
     $$\mathrm{Var}(x_{t,0}) = \sigma_W^2 \left(t + \frac{t(t-1)(2t-1)}{6}\right) = \Theta(\sigma_W^2 t^3), \qquad \mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{lin}}(x_{t,0} \mid x_{t,1}) = \sigma_W^2 \left(t + \frac{t(t^2-1)}{12}\right) = \Theta(\sigma_W^2 t^3). \tag{2.2}$$
     *(Under uncorrelated-but-dependent non-Gaussian noise, nonlinear conditional variance can differ in lower-order terms such as at $t = 1$, whereas for any i.i.d. sequence $\{W_s\}$ with $\mathrm{Var}(W_{s,1}) > 0$ the nonlinear conditional variance still scales as $\Theta(t^3)$.)*
   - *For general $k \ge 1$ and arbitrary $H_t \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times (k-1)}$ (including the $k = 1$ random walk, where $H_t = 0$ gives $\mathrm{Var}(x_t) = \sigma_W^2 t$), $\lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Cov}(S_t)) = \Theta(\sigma_W^2 t)$ for all $t \ge 1$, so at $\tau = 0$, $\sup_{\|v\|=1} \mathcal{E}_{t,0}(v) \ge c_k \sigma_W^2 t$. Moreover, across $(t, \tau)$: either $\mathrm{range}(H_t) = \mathrm{span}\{e_1,\dots,e_{k-1}\}$ (dropping $x_{t,0}$, yielding $\mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(e_0) = \Theta(\sigma_W^2 t^{2k-1})$ for all $\tau \ge 0$), or $\mathrm{range}(H_t) \ne \mathrm{span}\{e_1,\dots,e_{k-1}\}$ (yielding $\mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(e_0) = \Omega(\sigma_W^2 t \tau^2)$ as $\tau \to \infty$, and $\sup_{\|v\|=1} \mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) = \Omega(\sigma_W^2 t (1 + \tau^{2(k-1)}))$ whenever $e_{k-1} \notin \mathrm{range}(H_t)$).*

*Proof.*
*(1)* By Lemma 1.1, $(J_k(\lambda)^{t+\tau} (\delta e_{k-1}))_0 = \delta \lambda^{t+\tau-k+1} \binom{t+\tau}{k-1}$ (and $|\cdot| = |\delta| \binom{t+\tau}{k-1}$ for $J_{2k}^{\mathbb{R}}(1,\theta)$). Since $t+\tau \ge k-1$, every factor in the falling factorial $(t+\tau)(t+\tau-1)\cdots(t+\tau-k+2)$ is at least $t+\tau+1-k$, giving $\binom{t+\tau}{k-1} \ge \frac{(t+\tau+1-k)^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}$.

*(2)* Let $\Sigma_W = \sigma_W^2 I_k$ (if $\Sigma_W \succeq \sigma_W^2 I_k$, covariances dominate in Loewner order). With $S_0 = 0$, $S_t = \sum_{s=0}^{t-1} J_k(\lambda)^{t-1-s} W_s$, so $\mathrm{Cov}(S_t) = \sigma_W^2 \sum_{m=0}^{t-1} J_k(\lambda)^m (J_k(\lambda)^\top)^m$.
- The $(0,0)$-entry is $\mathrm{Var}(x_{t,0}) = \sigma_W^2 \sum_{m=0}^{t-1} \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \binom{m}{j}^2 \ge \sigma_W^2 \sum_{m=0}^{t-1} \binom{m}{k-1}^2 = \Theta(\sigma_W^2 t^{2k-1})$ (using $\sum_{m=0}^{t-1} m^2 = \frac{t(t-1)(2t-1)}{6}$ for $k=2$, and $\sum_{m=0}^{t-1} 1 = t$ for $k=1$).
- For $k=2$ ($J_2(1)$), $\mathrm{Var}(x_{t,1}) = \sigma_W^2 t$ and $\mathrm{Cov}(x_{t,0}, x_{t,1}) = \sigma_W^2 \sum_{m=0}^{t-1} m = \sigma_W^2 \frac{t(t-1)}{2}$. By the Schur complement formula for the linear-MMSE (and Gaussian conditional) variance,
  $$\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{lin}}(x_{t,0} \mid x_{t,1}) = \mathrm{Var}(x_{t,0}) - \frac{\mathrm{Cov}(x_{t,0}, x_{t,1})^2}{\mathrm{Var}(x_{t,1})} = \sigma_W^2 \left(t + \frac{t(t-1)(2t-1)}{6} - \frac{t(t-1)^2}{4}\right) = \sigma_W^2 \left(t + \frac{t(t^2-1)}{12}\right),$$
  proving (2.2). More generally for size $k$, $x_{t,0}$ contains $\sum_{s=0}^{t-1} \binom{t-1-s}{k-1} W_{s, k-1}$ (a degree-$(k-1)$ polynomial in $s$), whereas $(x_{t,1}, \dots, x_{t,k-1})$ constrain only $k-1$ linear combinations of $\{W_{s,k-1}\}_{s=0}^{t-1}$ (polynomials of degree $\le k-2$). By discrete Legendre polynomial orthogonality on $\{0, \dots, t-1\}$, the residual squared $\ell_2$-norm of $\binom{t-1-s}{k-1}$ orthogonal to $\mathrm{span}\{1, s, \dots, s^{k-2}\}$ is $\Theta(t^{2k-1})$, so $\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{lin}}(x_{t,0} \mid x_{t,1}, \dots, x_{t,k-1}) = \Theta(\sigma_W^2 t^{2k-1})$ (and by independence of $\{W_s\}$ across $s$, the same $\Theta(\sigma_W^2 t^{2k-1})$ order holds for nonlinear conditional variance when $W_s$ are i.i.d. with $\mathrm{Var}(W_{s,k-1}) > 0$).
- Next we prove $\lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Cov}(S_t)) = \Theta(\sigma_W^2 t)$ for every $k \ge 1$ and $t \ge 1$. Testing $v = e_{k-1}$ immediately gives $e_{k-1}^\top \mathrm{Cov}(S_t) e_{k-1} = \sigma_W^2 \sum_{m=0}^{t-1} |\lambda^m|^2 = \sigma_W^2 t$, so $\lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Cov}(S_t)) \le \sigma_W^2 t$. For the matching lower bound, take any unit vector $v \in \mathbb{S}^{k-1}$ and examine the $(k-1)$-th coordinate of $(J_k(\lambda)^\top)^m v$. By Lemma 1.1(a), the $(i, k-1)$ entry of $J_k(\lambda)^m$ is $\binom{m}{k-1-i}\lambda^{m-(k-1-i)}$ (taking $\binom{m}{\ell} := 0$ for $m < \ell$), so
  $$\big((J_k(\lambda)^\top)^m v\big)_{k-1} = \lambda^m \sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1} \tilde{v}_\ell \binom{m}{\ell} = \lambda^m q_v(m), \qquad \tilde{v}_\ell := \lambda^{-\ell} v_{k-1-\ell},$$
  where $\|\tilde{v}\|_2 = \|v\|_2 = 1$ (since $|\lambda| = 1$) and $q_v(x) := \sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1} \tilde{v}_\ell \frac{x(x-1)\cdots(x-\ell+1)}{\ell!}$ is a polynomial of degree at most $k-1$ agreeing with $\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1} \tilde{v}_\ell \binom{m}{\ell}$ at every integer $m \ge 0$. Expanding the falling factorial in the monomial basis via the signed Stirling numbers of the first kind $s(\ell, j)$, $\frac{x(x-1)\cdots(x-\ell+1)}{\ell!} = \sum_{j=0}^\ell \frac{s(\ell, j)}{\ell!} x^j$, and rescaling $x = t s$ ($s = m/t \in [0, 1]$ for $t \ge 1$) yields
  $$q_v(t s) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \alpha_j s^j, \qquad \alpha := D_t B \tilde{v},$$
  where $D_t := \mathrm{diag}(1, t, \dots, t^{k-1})$ and $B \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}$ is upper-triangular with entries $B_{j,\ell} = s(\ell, j)/\ell!$ ($0 \le j \le \ell \le k-1$) and positive diagonal $B_{\ell,\ell} = 1/\ell! > 0$. Thus $B$ is invertible with $\sigma_{\min}(B) > 0$ depending only on $k$. Because $D_t$ acts on the **left** and $t \ge 1$, every diagonal entry of $D_t$ is at least $1$, so uniformly over all $t \ge 1$ and $v \in \mathbb{S}^{k-1}$:
  $$\|\alpha\|_2 = \|D_t B \tilde{v}\|_2 \ge \|B \tilde{v}\|_2 \ge \sigma_{\min}(B) \|\tilde{v}\|_2 = \sigma_{\min}(B) > 0.$$
  Set $\hat{\alpha} := \alpha / \|\alpha\|_2 \in \mathbb{S}^{k-1}$ and $P_{\hat{\alpha}}(s) := \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \hat{\alpha}_j s^j$. For $\hat{\alpha} \in \mathbb{S}^{k-1}$ and $s \in [0, 1]$, $|P_{\hat{\alpha}}(s)| \le \sqrt{k}$ and $|P'_{\hat{\alpha}}(s)| \le k^{3/2}$, so $s \mapsto P_{\hat{\alpha}}(s)^2$ is uniformly Lipschitz on $[0, 1]$ with constant $L_k := 2k^2$. Hence the Riemann sums $F_t(\hat{\alpha}) := \frac{1}{t}\sum_{m=0}^{t-1} P_{\hat{\alpha}}(m/t)^2$ converge uniformly on the compact sphere $\mathbb{S}^{k-1}$ to $F_\infty(\hat{\alpha}) := \int_0^1 P_{\hat{\alpha}}(s)^2\,ds$:
  $$\sup_{\hat{\alpha} \in \mathbb{S}^{k-1}} \big|F_t(\hat{\alpha}) - F_\infty(\hat{\alpha})\big| \le \frac{L_k}{t} \xrightarrow{t \to \infty} 0.$$
  Because every nonzero polynomial of degree $\le k-1$ has positive $L^2([0,1])$ norm, compactness of $\mathbb{S}^{k-1}$ gives $c_\infty := \min_{\hat{\alpha} \in \mathbb{S}^{k-1}} F_\infty(\hat{\alpha}) > 0$. For $t \ge t_0 := \lceil 2 L_k / c_\infty \rceil$, $F_t(\hat{\alpha}) \ge c_\infty / 2$ for all $\hat{\alpha} \in \mathbb{S}^{k-1}$; for each integer $k \le t < t_0$, $P_{\hat{\alpha}}$ cannot vanish on all $t \ge k$ distinct nodes $\{0/t, \dots, (t-1)/t\}$, so $\min_{\hat{\alpha} \in \mathbb{S}^{k-1}} F_t(\hat{\alpha}) > 0$ by compactness; and for $1 \le t < k$, the $m = 0$ summand gives $\mathrm{Cov}(S_t) \succeq \sigma_W^2 I_k \succeq \frac{\sigma_W^2}{k} t I_k$. Combining all $t \ge 1$ produces a constant $c_k > 0$ depending only on $k$ such that
  $$v^\top \mathrm{Cov}(S_t) v \ge \sigma_W^2 \sum_{m=0}^{t-1} |q_v(m)|^2 = \sigma_W^2 t \|\alpha\|_2^2 F_t(\hat{\alpha}) \ge c_k \sigma_W^2 t \qquad \forall v \in \mathbb{S}^{k-1},\; \forall t \ge 1.$$
  Hence $\lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Cov}(S_t)) = \Theta(\sigma_W^2 t)$. (For the complex-conjugate block $J_{2k}^{\mathbb{R}}(1,\theta)$, the $(k-1)$-th $2\times 2$ block of $(J_{2k}^{\mathbb{R}}(1,\theta)^\top)^m v$ equals $R(1, -m\theta)\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1} \binom{m}{\ell} R(1, \ell\theta) v_{k-1-\ell}$, whose Euclidean norm matches $\|\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\binom{m}{\ell}\tilde{v}_\ell\|_2$ with $\tilde{v}_\ell := R(1, \ell\theta) v_{k-1-\ell} \in \mathbb{R}^2$, $\sum_\ell \|\tilde{v}_\ell\|_2^2 = 1$, so applying the same bound componentwise yields $\lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Cov}(S_t)) = \Theta(\sigma_W^2 t)$ as well.)
- Finally, for any $H_t \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times (k-1)}$ and $M_t = H_t^\top S_t$, set $\Sigma := \mathrm{Cov}(S_t) \succeq c_k \sigma_W^2 t I_k \succ 0$. The linear-MMSE residual covariance $P_{\mathrm{res}} := \Sigma - \Sigma H_t (H_t^\top \Sigma H_t)^+ H_t^\top \Sigma$ satisfies $P_{\mathrm{res}} = \Sigma^{1/2}(I_k - \Pi_{\Sigma^{1/2} H_t})\Sigma^{1/2}$, where $\Pi_{\Sigma^{1/2} H_t}$ is the orthogonal projector onto $\mathrm{range}(\Sigma^{1/2} H_t)$. Therefore, for every $w \in \mathbb{R}^k$,
  $$w^\top P_{\mathrm{res}} w = \min_{z \in \mathbb{R}^{k-1}} \|\Sigma^{1/2}(w - H_t z)\|_2^2 \ge \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma) \, \mathrm{dist}(w, \mathrm{range}(H_t))^2 \ge c_k \sigma_W^2 t \, \mathrm{dist}(w, \mathrm{range}(H_t))^2.$$
  At $\tau = 0$, choosing a unit vector $w \in \ker(H_t^\top)$ ($\mathrm{dist}(w, \mathrm{range}(H_t)) = 1$) gives $\mathcal{E}_{t,0}(w) \ge c_k \sigma_W^2 t$. For general $\tau \ge 0$, setting $v = e_0$ gives $w_\tau := (J_k(\lambda)^\top)^\tau e_0 = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \binom{\tau}{j} \lambda^{\tau - j} e_j$. Because $\{1, \binom{\tau}{1}, \dots, \binom{\tau}{k-1}\}$ are linearly independent polynomials in $\tau$, if $\mathrm{range}(H_t) \ne \mathrm{span}\{e_1,\dots,e_{k-1}\}$ there exists a unit vector $\eta \in \ker(H_t^\top)$ not proportional to $e_0$, so $|\eta^\top w_\tau|$ is a polynomial in $\tau$ of degree $d \ge 1$ and $\mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(e_0) \ge c_k \sigma_W^2 t \, |\eta^\top w_\tau|^2 = \Omega(\sigma_W^2 t \tau^{2d}) = \Omega(\sigma_W^2 t \tau^2)$ (and specifically $\Omega(\sigma_W^2 t \tau^{2(k-1)})$ whenever $e_{k-1} \notin \mathrm{range}(H_t)$, since $\mathrm{dist}(w_\tau, \mathrm{range}(H_t)) \ge \binom{\tau}{k-1}\mathrm{dist}(e_{k-1}, \mathrm{range}(H_t)) - O(\tau^{k-2})$); whereas if $\mathrm{range}(H_t) = \mathrm{span}\{e_1,\dots,e_{k-1}\}$, then $\mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(e_0) = \mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{lin}}(x_{t,0} \mid x_{t,1},\dots,x_{t,k-1}) = \Theta(\sigma_W^2 t^{2k-1})$ for all $\tau \ge 0$. $\square$

### 2.2 Data-Rate Sub-Criticality ($b < H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$)

Now allow **arbitrary** (possibly nonlinear, non-Markov) causal encoders sending $b$ bits per step: $Q_t \in \mathcal{Q}_t$ with $|\mathcal{Q}_t| \le 2^b$, and $M_t = \psi_t(M_{t-1}, Q_t)$ (so $M_t$ is a function of $(M_0, Q_1, \dots, Q_t)$).

**Theorem 2.3 (Nair–Evans / Tatikonda–Mitter Differential-Entropy Lower Bound).**
*Assume $E^u \ne \{0\}$ ($d_u \ge 1$, $H_{\mathrm{top}}(A) = \log_2 |\det A_u| > 0$), $S_0^u$ has finite differential entropy $h(S_0^u) > -\infty$ (base-2) and finite second moment, and $W_t^u$ is independent of $(S_0^u, W_{0:t-1}^u, V_{0:t}, M_t)$ (including $W_t^u = 0$ as a valid special case). Then for any causal $b$-bit encoder and any measurable estimator $\hat{S}_t^u(M_t)$:*
$$\mathbb{E}\big[\|S_t^u - \hat{S}_t^u(M_t)\|_2^2\big] \ge \frac{d_u}{2\pi e} \, 2^{\frac{2}{d_u} h(S_0^u \mid M_0)} \cdot 2^{\frac{2t}{d_u}(H_{\mathrm{top}}(A) - b)}. \tag{2.3}$$
*(If $M_0$ also uses at most $b$ bits, $h(S_0^u \mid M_0) \ge h(S_0^u) - b$.) Moreover, under observability of $(A_{u/c}, C_{u/c})$ with Kalman tracking error $\sup_t \mathbb{E}[\|S_t^u - \hat{S}_{t|t}^u\|_2^2] \le C_{\mathrm{Kal}} < \infty$, whenever $b < H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$ the late-query excess MSE diverges exponentially in both $t$ and $\tau$:*
$$\max_{1 \le j \le d_u} \mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}\!\left(\frac{\Pi_u^\top e_j}{\|\Pi_u^\top e_j\|_2}\right) \ge \frac{\sigma_{\min}(A_u^\tau)^2}{d_u \|\Pi_u\|_2^2} \left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[\|S_t^u - \hat{S}_t^u(M_t)\|_2^2\big] - C_{\mathrm{Kal}}\right) = \Omega\!\left(\sigma_{\min}(A_u^\tau)^2 \cdot 2^{\frac{2t}{d_u}(H_{\mathrm{top}}(A) - b)}\right). \tag{2.4}$$

*Proof.*
1. **Entropy-growth step**: Since $S_0^u$ has a Lebesgue density and $A_u$ is invertible, $A_u S_{t-1}^u$ has a density at every step $t \ge 1$, and adding independent noise $W_{t-1}^u$ preserves absolute continuity. Because conditioning reduces differential entropy and $W_{t-1}^u \perp (S_{t-1}^u, M_{t-1})$,
   $$\begin{aligned}
   h(S_t^u \mid M_{t-1}) &= h(A_u S_{t-1}^u + W_{t-1}^u \mid M_{t-1}) \\
   &\ge h(A_u S_{t-1}^u + W_{t-1}^u \mid M_{t-1}, W_{t-1}^u) \\
   &= h(A_u S_{t-1}^u \mid M_{t-1}) = h(S_{t-1}^u \mid M_{t-1}) + \log_2 |\det A_u| = h(S_{t-1}^u \mid M_{t-1}) + H_{\mathrm{top}}(A).
   \end{aligned}$$
2. **Rate-reduction step**: Since $M_t = \psi_t(M_{t-1}, Q_t)$ and $Q_t \in \mathcal{Q}_t$ is discrete with $|\mathcal{Q}_t| \le 2^b$, the data-processing inequality and nonnegativity of discrete conditional entropy give
   $$h(S_t^u \mid M_{t-1}) - h(S_t^u \mid M_t) \le h(S_t^u \mid M_{t-1}) - h(S_t^u \mid M_{t-1}, Q_t) = I(S_t^u; Q_t \mid M_{t-1}) \le H(Q_t \mid M_{t-1}) \le \log_2 |\mathcal{Q}_t| \le b.$$
   Combining Steps 1 and 2 yields $h(S_t^u \mid M_t) \ge h(S_{t-1}^u \mid M_{t-1}) + H_{\mathrm{top}}(A) - b$. Inducting from $0$ to $t$ gives
   $$h(S_t^u \mid M_t) \ge h(S_0^u \mid M_0) + t\big(H_{\mathrm{top}}(A) - b\big). \tag{2.5}$$
3. **Gaussian Max-Entropy + AM–GM**: Let $E_t := S_t^u - \hat{S}_t^u(M_t)$ and $\tilde{\Sigma}_t := \mathbb{E}[E_t E_t^\top] \succeq \mathrm{Cov}(E_t)$. By translation invariance of differential entropy given $M_t$ and conditioning reducing entropy, $h(S_t^u \mid M_t) = h(E_t \mid M_t) \le h(E_t)$. Letting $\phi_{\tilde{\Sigma}_t}$ be the density of $\mathcal{N}(0, \tilde{\Sigma}_t)$ (if $\tilde{\Sigma}_t$ is singular, the MSE is already unbounded or $h(E_t) = -\infty$, contradicting (2.5)),
   $$0 \le D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_{E_t} \,\|\, \phi_{\tilde{\Sigma}_t}) = \frac{1}{2}\log_2\!\big((2\pi e)^{d_u} \det \tilde{\Sigma}_t\big) - h(E_t)$$
   (using $\mathbb{E}[E_t^\top \tilde{\Sigma}_t^{-1} E_t] = \mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{\Sigma}_t^{-1} \mathbb{E}[E_t E_t^\top]) = d_u$). Applying AM–GM to the eigenvalues $\mu_1, \dots, \mu_{d_u} \ge 0$ of $\tilde{\Sigma}_t$,
   $$(\det \tilde{\Sigma}_t)^{1/d_u} = \left(\prod_{i=1}^{d_u} \mu_i\right)^{1/d_u} \le \frac{1}{d_u}\mathrm{Tr}(\tilde{\Sigma}_t) = \frac{1}{d_u}\mathbb{E}\big[\|S_t^u - \hat{S}_t^u(M_t)\|_2^2\big].$$
   Hence $h(S_t^u \mid M_t) \le \frac{d_u}{2}\log_2\!\big(\frac{2\pi e}{d_u}\mathbb{E}[\|S_t^u - \hat{S}_t^u(M_t)\|_2^2]\big)$, which rearranges with (2.5) to (2.3).
4. **Transfer to $\mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}$**: By $\|a+b\|_2^2 \le 2\|a\|_2^2 + 2\|b\|_2^2$, $\mathbb{E}[\|\hat{S}_{t|t}^u - \hat{S}_t^u(M_t)\|_2^2] \ge \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[\|S_t^u - \hat{S}_t^u(M_t)\|_2^2] - C_{\mathrm{Kal}}$, where $\hat{S}_t^u(M_t) := \mathbb{E}[\hat{S}_{t|t}^u \mid M_t]$ achieves the optimal MSE. Since $\|y\|_2 \le \|A_u^{-\tau}\|_2 \|A_u^\tau y\|_2 = \sigma_{\min}(A_u^\tau)^{-1} \|A_u^\tau y\|_2$ for all $y \in \mathbb{R}^{d_u}$, summing over the standard basis $e_1, \dots, e_{d_u}$ of $\mathbb{R}^{d_u}$ and setting $v_j := \Pi_u^\top e_j / \|\Pi_u^\top e_j\|_2$ yields (2.4). $\square$

**Theorem 2.4 (Deterministic Finite-Alphabet Pigeonhole Lower Bound).**
*In the noiseless scalar system $s_{t+1} = \lambda s_t + c_t$ with $|\lambda| > 1$ and $K = 2^b \in \mathbb{N}$ ($1 \le K < |\lambda|$), suppose initial states $x_i := i \frac{\Delta}{K^t}$ ($0 \le i \le K^t$, $\Delta > 0$) are compressed at time $t$ into a transcript/summary $\mathrm{enc} : \{0,\dots,K^t\} \to [K^t]$. Then for any decoder $\mathrm{dec} : [K^t] \to \mathbb{R}$, there exists $i \in \{0,\dots,K^t\}$ such that*
$$\big|\mathrm{dec}(\mathrm{enc}(i)) - s_t(i)\big| \ge \frac{\Delta}{2} \left(\frac{|\lambda|}{K}\right)^t \xrightarrow{t \to \infty} \infty, \tag{2.6}$$
*and at horizon $t + \tau$, worst-case error is at least $\frac{\Delta}{2} |\lambda|^\tau (|\lambda|/K)^t$.*

*Proof.* Since $K^t + 1 > K^t$, the pigeonhole principle yields $i \ne j$ with $\mathrm{enc}(i) = \mathrm{enc}(j) =: m$. Then $|x_i - x_j| \ge \Delta / K^t$, so $|s_t(i) - s_t(j)| = |\lambda|^t |x_i - x_j| \ge \Delta (|\lambda|/K)^t$. By the triangle inequality, $\Delta (|\lambda|/K)^t \le |s_t(i) - \mathrm{dec}(m)| + |\mathrm{dec}(m) - s_t(j)|$, so the larger of the two errors is at least $\frac{\Delta}{2}(|\lambda|/K)^t$. $\square$

### 2.3 The $|\lambda| = 1$ Zero-Topological-Entropy Edge Case

Because $H_{\mathrm{top}}(A) = \sum_{|\lambda_i| > 1} \log_2 |\lambda_i|$ assigns **zero** bits/step to marginal modes ($|\lambda| = 1$), the threshold $b = H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$ exhibits a sharp split governed by noise and coder architecture:
1. **$b = H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$ with persistent marginal noise $W_t^c \ne 0$**: Zero spare bits remain for $E^c$, so $\mathrm{Var}(S_t^c \mid M_t) = \mathrm{Var}(S_t^c) = \Theta(t^{2k-1})$ diverges polynomially.
2. **$b > H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$ in a causal differential/zooming predictive coder** (decoder maintains an unbounded state $\hat{S}_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ updated by $b$ bits/step): Allocating $\delta > 0$ spare bits/step to $E^c$ stabilizes bounded or Gaussian noise so $\sup_{t \ge 0} \mathbb{E}[\|S_t - \hat{S}_t\|_2^2] < \infty$ (Nair–Evans 2004, Thm. 2.1). Explicitly, for $s_{t+1} = s_t + w_t$ with $|w_t| \le W$ and $K = 2^{\delta T_0} \ge 2$ levels transmitted every $T_0$ steps, if $|s_{mT_0} - \hat{s}_{mT_0}| \le L_m$, then $s_{(m+1)T_0} - \hat{s}_{mT_0} \in [-(L_m + T_0 W), L_m + T_0 W]$; partitioning into $K$ equal subintervals gives $L_{m+1} = \frac{L_m + T_0 W}{K} \to \frac{T_0 W}{K - 1} < \infty$.
3. **Fixed $2^b$-state memory register ($|\mathcal{M}_t| \le 2^b$, no external real accumulator)**: In contrast to (2), if the register itself has at most $2^b$ states at time $t$, then for $S_{t+1} = S_t + W_t$ with $W_t \stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ ($S_0 = 0$), we have $S_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 t)$, $h(S_t) = \frac{1}{2}\log_2(2\pi e \sigma^2 t)$, and $I(S_t; M_t) \le H(M_t) \le b$. Hence $h(S_t \mid M_t) \ge \frac{1}{2}\log_2(2\pi e \sigma^2 t) - b$, and the 1D Gaussian max-entropy bound gives
   $$\mathbb{E}\big[(S_t - \hat{S}_t(M_t))^2\big] \ge \frac{1}{2\pi e} \, 2^{2 h(S_t \mid M_t)} \ge \sigma^2 t \cdot 2^{-2b} = \Omega(\sigma^2 t \cdot 2^{-2b}) \xrightarrow{t \to \infty} \infty \quad \forall b < \infty. \tag{2.7}$$
   Thus a fixed $2^b$-state memory register *always* diverges linearly on a noisy marginal random walk regardless of $b$.

## 3. Super-Critical Regime ($d_{\mathrm{mem}} \ge r$ and Sufficient Bit-Rate)

### 3.1 Zero-Decay Continuity Phase under Full-State or Decoupled ($C_s = 0$) Observations

**Theorem 3.1 (Uniform Boundedness & Exponential $\tau$-Decay for $d_{\mathrm{mem}} \ge r$).**
*Suppose $d_{\mathrm{mem}} \ge r$, $\mathbb{E}[\|S_0^s\|_2^2] < \infty$, and either (i) full-state observation $X_t = S_t$ holds, or (ii) decoupled partial observation $X_t = C_{u/c} S_t^{u/c} + V_t$ ($C_s = 0$) holds. Let $M_t \in \mathbb{R}^r$ track the exact $E^{u/c}$ sufficient statistic:*
- *Under $X_t = S_t$: $M_t := \Pi_{u/c} X_t = S_t^{u/c}$, updated Markovianly by $M_{t+1} = \Pi_{u/c} X_{t+1}$.*
- *Under $C_s = 0$ (for jointly Gaussian noise, or using the linear-MMSE Kalman filter summary): $M_t := \hat{S}_{t|t}^{u/c} = \mathbb{E}[S_t^{u/c} \mid X_{0:t}]$ (or $\hat{S}_{t|t}^{\mathrm{lin},\, u/c}$ in the non-Gaussian linear-MMSE case), updated Markovianly in $\mathbb{R}^r$ via the $r$-dimensional Kalman filter on $(A_{u/c}, C_{u/c})$ (with deterministic time-varying gains $K_t$, or steady-state gain $K_\infty$ under observability and $\Sigma_V \succ 0$).*

*With the linear late-query predictor $\hat{y}_{t,\tau}(v) := v^\top P_{u/c} A_{u/c}^\tau M_t$, for any $\bar{\rho} \in (\rho(A_s), 1)$ there exists $C_{\mathrm{stab}} \ge 1$ such that for ALL $t, \tau \in \mathbb{N}_0$:*
$$\sup_{v \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}} \mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) \le \|P_s\|_2^2 \, C_{\mathrm{stab}}^4 \, \bar{\rho}^{2\tau} \left(\mathbb{E}[\|S_0^s\|_2^2] + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_W^s)}{1 - \bar{\rho}^2}\right) \le \|P_s\|_2^2 \, C_{\mathrm{stab}}^4 \left(\mathbb{E}[\|S_0^s\|_2^2] + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_W^s)}{1 - \bar{\rho}^2}\right) < \infty. \tag{3.1}$$
*In particular, the excess MSE (over $\hat{S}_{t|t}$ when jointly Gaussian, or over the linear-MMSE Kalman oracle $\hat{S}_{t|t}^{\mathrm{lin}}$ in general) is uniformly bounded in $(t, \tau)$ and decays exponentially to $0$ as $\tau \to \infty$ (**zero-decay continuity phase**).*

*Proof.* Because $A^\tau P_{u/c} = P_{u/c} A_{u/c}^\tau$ and $A^\tau P_s = P_s A_s^\tau$, we have $v^\top A^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t} = v^\top P_{u/c} A_{u/c}^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t}^{u/c} + v^\top P_s A_s^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t}^s$ (with $\hat{S}_{t|t}$ denoting the conditional expectation under Gaussian noise, or the linear-MMSE projection in general). In both (i) and (ii), $M_t = \hat{S}_{t|t}^{u/c}$ identically, so the $E^{u/c}$ error cancels completely:
$$\hat{y}_{t,\tau}(v) - v^\top A^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t} = -v^\top P_s A_s^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t}^s.$$
By Proposition 1.2 and Cauchy–Schwarz,
$$\mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) = \mathbb{E}\big[(v^\top P_s A_s^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t}^s)^2\big] \le \|v\|_2^2 \|P_s\|_2^2 \|A_s^\tau\|_2^2 \, \mathbb{E}\big[\|\hat{S}_{t|t}^s\|_2^2\big].$$
Even if $\Sigma_W$ correlates $W_t^{u/c}$ and $W_t^s$, Jensen's inequality for conditional expectation (and $L^2$-orthogonality of linear-MMSE projection) gives $\mathbb{E}[\|\hat{S}_{t|t}^s\|_2^2] \le \mathbb{E}[\|S_t^s\|_2^2]$. Since $\rho(A_s) < \bar{\rho} < 1$, Gelfand's formula (or Lemma 1.1 on the Jordan blocks of $A_s$) supplies $C_{\mathrm{stab}} \ge 1$ with $\|A_s^k\|_2 \le C_{\mathrm{stab}} \bar{\rho}^k$ for all $k \ge 0$. Expanding $S_t^s = A_s^t S_0^s + \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} A_s^{t-1-k} W_k^s$ and using mutual independence of $S_0^s, W_0^s, \dots, W_{t-1}^s$ (with $\mathbb{E}[W_k^s] = 0$),
$$\mathbb{E}\big[\|S_t^s\|_2^2\big] = \mathbb{E}\big[\|A_s^t S_0^s\|_2^2\big] + \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} \mathbb{E}\big[\|A_s^{t-1-k} W_k^s\|_2^2\big] \le C_{\mathrm{stab}}^2 \bar{\rho}^{2t} \mathbb{E}[\|S_0^s\|_2^2] + C_{\mathrm{stab}}^2 \mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_W^s) \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} \bar{\rho}^{2k} \le C_{\mathrm{stab}}^2 \left(\mathbb{E}[\|S_0^s\|_2^2] + \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma_W^s)}{1 - \bar{\rho}^2}\right).$$
Multiplying by $\|A_s^\tau\|_2^2 \le C_{\mathrm{stab}}^2 \bar{\rho}^{2\tau}$ yields (3.1). (Under full-state $X_t = S_t$ with optimal decoder $\mathbb{E}[S_t^s \mid M_t]$, $\mathbb{E}[\|S_t^s - \mathbb{E}[S_t^s \mid M_t]\|_2^2] \le \mathbb{E}[\|S_t^s\|_2^2]$ satisfies the same bound.) $\square$

### 3.2 Coupled Partial Observation ($C_s \ne 0$) Subtlety

Suppose $X_t = C_{u/c} S_t^{u/c} + C_s S_t^s + V_t$ with $C_s \ne 0$, and $(A_{u/c}, C_{u/c})$ is observable.

**Proposition 3.2 (Luenberger Observer at $d_{\mathrm{mem}} = r$ vs. Full Kalman Oracle for $C_s \ne 0$).**
1. **Uniform tracking as $t \to \infty$ (fixed $\tau$)**: *Because $(A_{u/c}, C_{u/c})$ is observable and $A_{u/c}$ is invertible, $(A_{u/c}, C_{u/c} A_{u/c})$ is observable, so there exists a gain $L \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times m}$ with $\rho\big((I_r - L C_{u/c}) A_{u/c}\big) < 1$. Updating $M_t \in \mathbb{R}^r$ by the $r$-dimensional Luenberger observer*
   $$M_{t+1} = A_{u/c} M_t + L\big(X_{t+1} - C_{u/c} A_{u/c} M_t\big) \tag{3.2}$$
   *achieves $\sup_{t \ge 0} \mathbb{E}[\|S_t^{u/c} - M_t\|_2^2] < \infty$, and hence $\sup_{t \ge 0} \sup_{\|v\|=1} \mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) \le C(\tau) < \infty$ for every fixed $\tau \ge 0$.*
2. **Honest $\tau \to \infty$ caveat**: *Relative to the full-$d$-dimensional Kalman filter $\hat{S}_{t|t}$ (which tracks $\hat{S}_{t|t}^s$ to de-noise $C_s S_t^s$ inside $X_t$), any nonzero $O(1)$ discrepancy $\delta_t^{u/c} := \hat{S}_{t|t}^{u/c} - M_t$ is multiplied by $A_{u/c}^\tau$ in $\mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) = \mathbb{E}[(v^\top P_{u/c} A_{u/c}^\tau \delta_t^{u/c} + v^\top P_s A_s^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t}^s)^2]$. Thus, if $E^u \ne \{0\}$ and $C_s \ne 0$, achieving simultaneous uniform boundedness as **both** $t \to \infty$ and $\tau \to \infty$ against $\hat{S}_{t|t}$ requires tracking the coupled Kalman state (dimension up to $d$), whereas $d_{\mathrm{mem}} = r$ suffices for uniform $O(1)$ tracking as $t \to \infty$ at fixed $\tau$.*

*Proof.* Set $e_t := S_t^{u/c} - M_t$ and $F := (I_r - L C_{u/c}) A_{u/c}$. By Hautus's PBH test, $\mathrm{rank}\begin{pmatrix} \lambda I_r - A_{u/c} \\ C_{u/c} A_{u/c} \end{pmatrix} = \mathrm{rank}\begin{pmatrix} \lambda I_r - A_{u/c} \\ C_{u/c} \end{pmatrix} = r$ for all $\lambda \ne 0$ (since $A_{u/c}$ is invertible, and at $\lambda = 0$, $\mathrm{rank}(A_{u/c}) = r$), so $(A_{u/c}, C_{u/c} A_{u/c})$ is observable and pole placement produces $L$ with $\rho(F) < 1$. Substituting $X_{t+1} = C_{u/c}(A_{u/c} S_t^{u/c} + W_t^{u/c}) + C_s S_{t+1}^s + V_{t+1}$ into (3.2) yields
$$e_{t+1} = F e_t + (I_r - L C_{u/c}) W_t^{u/c} - L C_s S_{t+1}^s - L V_{t+1}.$$
Because $\rho(F) < 1$, $\|F^k\|_2 \le C_F \bar{\rho}_F^k$ for some $\bar{\rho}_F \in (\rho(F), 1)$. Since $\sup_{t \ge 0} \mathbb{E}[\|S_{t+1}^s\|_2^2] < \infty$ (Theorem 3.1), applying Minkowski's $L^2$-triangle inequality to $e_t = F^t e_0 + \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} F^{t-1-k} \xi_k$ (with $\sup_k \mathbb{E}[\|\xi_k\|_2^2]^{1/2} =: M_\xi < \infty$) yields $\mathbb{E}[\|e_t\|_2^2]^{1/2} \le C_F \bar{\rho}_F^t \|e_0\|_{L^2} + \frac{C_F M_\xi}{1 - \bar{\rho}_F} < \infty$ uniformly in $t$. Combining with $\sup_t \mathbb{E}[\|\hat{S}_{t|t}^{u/c} - S_t^{u/c}\|_2^2] < \infty$ gives $\sup_t \mathbb{E}[\|\delta_t^{u/c}\|_2^2] < \infty$, while $\tau \to \infty$ amplifies $\delta_t^{u/c}$ via $A_{u/c}^\tau$ whenever $\delta_t^{u/c}$ has nonzero projection onto $E^u$. $\square$

### 3.3 Finite Bit-Rate ($b > H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$) Adaptive Zooming Lattice Coder

**Theorem 3.3 (Sufficient Bit-Rate Stabilization).**
*Consider $S_{t+1}^{u/c} = A_{u/c} S_t^{u/c} + W_t^{u/c}$ with full-state observation and bounded process noise $\|W_t^{u/c}\|_\infty \le W_{\max}$.*
1. *In the scalar case $s_{t+1} = \lambda s_t + w_t$ with $|\lambda| > 1$, $|w_t| \le W$, and $K = 2^b > |\lambda|$ ($b > \log_2 |\lambda|$), the adaptive zooming quantizer maintaining $|s_t - \hat{s}_t| \le L_t$ via $K$-level uniform partitioning of $[-(|\lambda| L_t + W), |\lambda| L_t + W]$ satisfies $L_{t+1} = \frac{|\lambda| L_t + W}{K}$ and hence*
   $$|s_t - \hat{s}_t| \le L_t = \left(\frac{|\lambda|}{K}\right)^t L_0 + \frac{W}{K} \sum_{j=0}^{t-1} \left(\frac{|\lambda|}{K}\right)^j \le L_0 + \frac{W}{K - |\lambda|} < \infty \quad \forall t \ge 0. \tag{3.3}$$
2. *For general $A_{u/c}$ with $b > H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$, choosing a block period $T_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ large enough that $2^{\lfloor b T_0 \rfloor} > \prod_{\text{blocks } j} \big(\prod_{m=1}^{k_j} N_{j,m}\big)$ with rectangular lattice levels $N_{j,m}$ exceeding the $T_0$-step Jordan coordinate expansion (allocating $\delta_j > 0$ spare bits per block to $E^c$ and $> T_0 \log_2 |\lambda_j|$ to each $E^u$ block) yields $\sup_{t \ge 0} \|S_t^{u/c} - \hat{S}_t^{u/c}\|_2 < \infty$. (For unbounded Gaussian noise and observable $(A_{u/c}, C_{u/c})$, Nair–Evans 2004, Thm. 2.1 establishes $\sup_{t \ge 0} \mathbb{E}[\|S_t^{u/c} - \hat{S}_t^{u/c}\|_2^2] < \infty$ whenever $b > H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$.)*

*Caveat on open-loop $\tau \to \infty$ at finite bit-rate*: Because a $b$-bit coder with $W > 0$ retains a steady-state quantization residual $\|S_t^u - \hat{S}_t^u\| \asymp W/(K - |\lambda|)$, $\sup_{t \ge 0} \mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) < \infty$ holds for every **fixed** $\tau \ge 0$, whereas open-loop extrapolation to $\tau \to \infty$ without fresh bits during $(t, t+\tau]$ still multiplies that $O(1)$ quantization floor by $\|A_u^\tau\|_2^2$.

## 4. Discrete SDPI Exponential Forgetting & Causal-State Collapse (R2)

### 4.1 Dobrushin Contraction & Doeblin Minorization

Let $\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{Y}$ be finite sets and $K : \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{Y} \to [0, 1]$ a stochastic kernel ($\sum_{y \in \mathcal{Y}} K(x, y) = 1$ for all $x \in \mathcal{X}$). For $P, Q \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})$, define $(PK)(y) := \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} P(x) K(x, y)$, total variation $\mathrm{TV}(P, Q) := \frac{1}{2} \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} |P(x) - Q(x)| = \max_{A \subseteq \mathcal{X}} (P(A) - Q(A))$, and the **Dobrushin contraction coefficient**
$$\eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K) := \frac{1}{2} \max_{x, x' \in \mathcal{X}} \sum_{y \in \mathcal{Y}} |K(x, y) - K(x', y)| \in [0, 1]. \tag{4.1}$$

**Lemma 4.1 (Dobrushin TV Contraction & Doeblin Minorization).**
*(a) For any $P, Q \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})$, $\mathrm{TV}(PK, QK) \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K) \mathrm{TV}(P, Q)$, and $\sup_{P \ne Q} \frac{\mathrm{TV}(PK, QK)}{\mathrm{TV}(P, Q)} = \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K)$.*
*(b) If $\alpha : \mathcal{Y} \to \mathbb{R}$ satisfies $\alpha(y) \le K(x, y)$ for all $x, y$ (e.g., $\alpha(y) = \min_x K(x, y)$), then $\eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K) \le 1 - \sum_{y \in \mathcal{Y}} \alpha(y)$.*

*Proof.* *(a)* Let $\delta(x) := P(x) - Q(x)$, $\delta^+(x) := \max(\delta(x), 0)$, $\delta^-(x) := \max(-\delta(x), 0)$. Since $\sum_x \delta(x) = 0$ and $|\delta(x)| = \delta^+(x) + \delta^-(x)$, we have $T := \sum_x \delta^+(x) = \sum_x \delta^-(x) = \mathrm{TV}(P, Q)$. If $T = 0$, $P = Q$ and both sides are $0$. If $T > 0$, then
$$T\big((PK)(y) - (QK)(y)\big) = \left(\sum_{x'} \delta^-(x')\right)\sum_x \delta^+(x) K(x,y) - \left(\sum_x \delta^+(x)\right)\sum_{x'} \delta^-(x') K(x',y) = \sum_{x, x'} \delta^+(x)\delta^-(x')\big(K(x,y) - K(x',y)\big).$$
Taking absolute values, summing over $y$, multiplying by $\frac{1}{2}$, and bounding $\frac{1}{2}\sum_y |K(x,y) - K(x',y)| \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K)$ gives $T \cdot \mathrm{TV}(PK, QK) \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K) T^2$, i.e., $\mathrm{TV}(PK, QK) \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K) \mathrm{TV}(P, Q)$. Equality is attained by $P = \delta_x, Q = \delta_{x'}$.
*(b)* For any $x, x'$, $|K(x,y) - K(x',y)| = |(K(x,y) - \alpha(y)) - (K(x',y) - \alpha(y))| \le (K(x,y) - \alpha(y)) + (K(x',y) - \alpha(y))$; summing over $y$ gives $\sum_y |K(x,y) - K(x',y)| \le 2 - 2\sum_y \alpha(y)$. $\square$

### 4.2 Self-Contained Proof of $\eta_{\mathrm{KL}}(K) \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K)$ (Cohen–Kempermann–Zbăganu 1993)

Define the KL SDPI contraction coefficient $\eta_{\mathrm{KL}}(K) := \sup_{P \ne Q,\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P \| Q) < \infty} \frac{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(PK \,\|\, QK)}{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P \,\|\, Q)}$, where $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P \,\|\, Q) = \sum_{x} P(x) \ln \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$ (natural logarithm; ratios are base-invariant).

For $\gamma > 0$, define the **hockey-stick divergence** and **centered hockey-stick divergence**:
$$E_\gamma(P \,\|\, Q) := \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \big(P(x) - \gamma Q(x)\big)_+ = \max_{A \subseteq \mathcal{X}} \big(P(A) - \gamma Q(A)\big), \qquad \tilde{E}_\gamma(P \,\|\, Q) := E_\gamma(P \,\|\, Q) - (1 - \gamma)_+. \tag{4.2}$$
Notice that $\sum_x (P(x) - \gamma Q(x)) = 1 - \gamma$ and $z = (z)_+ - (-z)_+$ imply $\sum_x (P(x) - \gamma Q(x))_+ - \sum_x (\gamma Q(x) - P(x))_+ = 1 - \gamma$, so:
$$\tilde{E}_\gamma(P \,\|\, Q) = \begin{cases} \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \big(P(x) - \gamma Q(x)\big)_+ \ge 0 & \text{if } \gamma \ge 1, \\[4pt] \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \big(\gamma Q(x) - P(x)\big)_+ \ge 0 & \text{if } 0 < \gamma < 1. \end{cases} \tag{4.3}$$

**Lemma 4.2 (Integral Representation of KL Divergence).** *For all $P, Q \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})$,*
$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P \,\|\, Q) = \int_0^\infty \frac{1}{\gamma} \, \tilde{E}_\gamma(P \,\|\, Q) \, d\gamma. \tag{4.4}$$

*Proof.* By Tonelli's theorem (all summands in (4.3) are nonnegative), the integral equals $\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} I(P(x), Q(x))$, where
$$I(p, q) := \int_0^1 \frac{(\gamma q - p)_+}{\gamma} \, d\gamma + \int_1^\infty \frac{(p - \gamma q)_+}{\gamma} \, d\gamma.$$
- If $q = 0$ and $p > 0$: $(p - \gamma \cdot 0)_+ = p$ on $[1, \infty)$, so $I(p, 0) = p \int_1^\infty \frac{d\gamma}{\gamma} = +\infty$, matching $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P \,\|\, Q) = +\infty$.
- If $p = 0$ and $q \ge 0$: the $[1,\infty)$ integral is $0$ and the $(0,1]$ integral is $\int_0^1 q\, d\gamma = q = p \ln \frac{p}{q} - p + q$ (with $0 \ln 0 := 0$).
- If $p > 0, q > 0$:
  - When $p > q$: $\int_0^1 (\gamma q - p)_+/\gamma\, d\gamma = 0$, and $\int_1^{p/q} \big(\frac{p}{\gamma} - q\big)\, d\gamma = p \ln \frac{p}{q} - q(\frac{p}{q} - 1) = p\ln\frac{p}{q} - p + q$.
  - When $p \le q$: $\int_1^\infty (p - \gamma q)_+/\gamma\, d\gamma = 0$, and $\int_{p/q}^1 \big(q - \frac{p}{\gamma}\big)\, d\gamma = q(1 - \frac{p}{q}) - p \ln \frac{q}{p} = p\ln\frac{p}{q} - p + q$.
Summing over $x \in \mathcal{X}$ and using $\sum_x (Q(x) - P(x)) = 1 - 1 = 0$ yields $\sum_x I(P(x), Q(x)) = \sum_x P(x) \ln\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} = D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P \,\|\, Q)$. $\square$

**Theorem 4.3 (Cohen–Kempermann–Zbăganu Inequality $\eta_{\mathrm{KL}}(K) \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K)$).**
*For any finite-state stochastic kernel $K : \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{Y} \to [0, 1]$ and all $P, Q \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})$,*
$$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(PK \,\|\, QK) \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K) \, D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P \,\|\, Q), \qquad \text{hence } \eta_{\mathrm{KL}}(K) \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K). \tag{4.5}$$

*Proof.* Fix $\gamma > 0$ and any event $B \subseteq \mathcal{Y}$. Define $g(x) := \sum_{y \in B} K(x, y) \in [0, 1]$, $m := \min_{x \in \mathcal{X}} g(x)$, and $M := \max_{x \in \mathcal{X}} g(x)$. Picking $x_{\max}, x_{\min}$ achieving $M$ and $m$,
$$M - m = \sum_{y \in B} \big(K(x_{\max}, y) - K(x_{\min}, y)\big) \le \mathrm{TV}\big(K(x_{\max}, \cdot), K(x_{\min}, \cdot)\big) \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K).$$
Now evaluate $(PK)(B) - \gamma(QK)(B) = \sum_x g(x) (P(x) - \gamma Q(x))$:
- **Case $\gamma \ge 1$**: Since $\sum_x (P(x) - \gamma Q(x)) = 1 - \gamma \le 0$ and $m \ge 0$,
  $$(PK)(B) - \gamma(QK)(B) = m(1 - \gamma) + \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} (g(x) - m)(P(x) - \gamma Q(x)) \le 0 + (M - m)\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} (P(x) - \gamma Q(x))_+ \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K) \tilde{E}_\gamma(P \,\|\, Q).$$
  Taking $\max_{B \subseteq \mathcal{Y}}$ gives $\tilde{E}_\gamma(PK \,\|\, QK) = \max_B ((PK)(B) - \gamma(QK)(B))_+ \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K) \tilde{E}_\gamma(P \,\|\, Q)$.
- **Case $0 < \gamma < 1$**: Using $\sum_x (P(x) - \gamma Q(x)) = 1 - \gamma$, rewrite
  $$(PK)(B) - \gamma(QK)(B) - (1 - \gamma) = (1 - M)(\gamma - 1) + \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} (M - g(x))(\gamma Q(x) - P(x)).$$
  (Indeed, $(1-M)(\gamma-1) + M(\gamma - 1) - \sum_x g(x)(\gamma Q(x) - P(x)) = (\gamma - 1) + \sum_x g(x)(P(x) - \gamma Q(x))$.) Since $1 - M \ge 0$ and $\gamma - 1 < 0$, the first term is $\le 0$; since $0 \le M - g(x) \le M - m \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K)$,
  $$(PK)(B) - \gamma(QK)(B) - (1 - \gamma) \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K) \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} (\gamma Q(x) - P(x))_+ = \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K) \tilde{E}_\gamma(P \,\|\, Q).$$
  Taking $\max_{B \subseteq \mathcal{Y}}$ yields $\tilde{E}_\gamma(PK \,\|\, QK) \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K) \tilde{E}_\gamma(P \,\|\, Q)$ for $0 < \gamma < 1$ as well.

Integrating $\tilde{E}_\gamma(PK \,\|\, QK) \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K) \tilde{E}_\gamma(P \,\|\, Q)$ against $\frac{1}{\gamma}\, d\gamma$ over $(0, \infty)$ via Lemma 4.2 gives (4.5). $\square$

### 4.3 Multi-Step SDPI Exponential Forgetting

**Theorem 4.4 (Multi-Step SDPI Exponential Forgetting).**
*For any finite-state Markov chain $S_0 \to M_0 \xrightarrow{K_1} M_1 \xrightarrow{K_2} \cdots \xrightarrow{K_t} M_t$,*
$$I(S_0; M_t) \le \left(\prod_{s=1}^t \eta_{\mathrm{KL}}(K_s)\right) I(S_0; M_0) \le \left(\prod_{s=1}^t \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K_s)\right) I(S_0; M_0), \tag{4.6}$$
*and similarly $\mathrm{TV}(P_{M_t \mid S_0 = a}, P_{M_t \mid S_0 = b}) \le \big(\prod_{s=1}^t \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K_s)\big) \mathrm{TV}(P_{M_0 \mid S_0 = a}, P_{M_0 \mid S_0 = b})$. Whenever $\sup_s \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K_s) \le \bar{\eta} < 1$ (e.g., via Doeblin minorization $\sum_y \min_x K_s(x, y) \ge \alpha > 0 \implies \bar{\eta} \le 1 - \alpha$), $I(S_0; M_t) \le \bar{\eta}^t I(S_0; M_0) \to 0$ exponentially.*

*Proof.* By the variational identity $I(S_0; M_s) = \mathbb{E}_{x_0 \sim P_{S_0}}\big[D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{M_s \mid S_0 = x_0} \,\|\, P_{M_s})\big]$ and the Markov property $P_{M_s \mid S_0 = x_0} = P_{M_{s-1} \mid S_0 = x_0} K_s$, $P_{M_s} = P_{M_{s-1}} K_s$, applying Theorem 4.3 inside the expectation yields $I(S_0; M_s) \le \eta_{\mathrm{KL}}(K_s) I(S_0; M_{s-1}) \le \eta_{\mathrm{TV}}(K_s) I(S_0; M_{s-1})$. Inducting from $s = 1$ to $t$ gives (4.6). $\square$

*Structural Remark on $M_{t+1} = f_t(M_t, X_{t+1})$*: Notice that when $X_{t+1}$ carries *fresh* observations of $S_{t+1}$, $S_0 \to M_t \to M_{t+1}$ is NOT Markov along the observation path $S_0 \to S_{t+1} \to X_{t+1} \to M_{t+1}$ unless either (i) no fresh $S_0$-informative observations arrive (pure iterative memory re-compression, or during the open-loop future horizon $[t, t+\tau]$), or (ii) $X_{t+1}$ is itself degraded through a contractive channel (such as the $E^s$ transition below). In both regimes, Theorem 4.4 governs the exact exponential forgetting rate.

### 4.4 Crutchfield Causal States ($\varepsilon$-Machine) & Asymptotic Manifold Collapse

In computational mechanics (Crutchfield & Young 1989), two observation pasts $x_{-\infty:t} \sim_\varepsilon x'_{-\infty:t}$ belong to the same **causal state** $\epsilon(x_{-\infty:t})$ iff they induce the same conditional law over future observations $\mathbb{P}(X_{t+1:\infty} \in \cdot \mid X_{-\infty:t})$.
- **Finite-horizon ($\tau \ge 1$) causal state**: For the linear-Gaussian model (1.1), $X_{t+\tau} = C A^\tau S_t + C \Delta_{t,\tau} + V_{t+\tau}$ is conditionally Gaussian given $X_{0:t}$ with mean $C A^\tau \hat{S}_{t|t}$ and deterministic (data-independent) covariance determined by $\Sigma_{t|t}$, $\Sigma_W$, $\Sigma_V$. Under observability of $(A, C)$, the minimal predictive sufficient statistic is $\hat{S}_{t|t}$ (a $d$-dimensional manifold).
- **Asymptotic late-query ($\tau \to \infty$) collapse**: Consider $\tau$-shifted future prediction under full-state or decoupled ($C_s = 0$) observations. On $E^s$, $S_t^s \to S_{t+\tau}^s = A_s^\tau S_t^s + \Delta_{t,\tau}^s$ is a contracting Gaussian channel. Assuming $\Sigma_W^s \succ 0$ ($\tau \ge 1$), $\mathrm{Cov}(\Delta_{t,\tau}^s) = \sum_{j=0}^{\tau-1} A_s^j \Sigma_W^s (A_s^\top)^j \succeq \Sigma_W^s \succeq \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_W^s) I_{d_s}$. Using $\ln \det(I + M) \le \mathrm{Tr}(M)$ for $M \succeq 0$ and $\|A_s^\tau\|_2 \le C_{\mathrm{stab}} \bar{\rho}^\tau$:
  $$I(S_t^s; S_{t+\tau}^s) = \frac{1}{2}\log_2 \det\!\Big(I_{d_s} + A_s^\tau \mathrm{Cov}(S_t^s)(A_s^\top)^\tau \big(\mathrm{Cov}(\Delta_{t,\tau}^s)\big)^{-1}\Big) \le \frac{C_{\mathrm{stab}}^2 \, \mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{Cov}(S_t^s))}{2 \ln 2 \, \lambda_{\min}(\Sigma_W^s)} \bar{\rho}^{2\tau} \xrightarrow{\tau \to \infty} 0. \tag{4.7}$$
  Hence all dependence of future states on $S_t^s$ decays exponentially to zero as $\tau \to \infty$, collapsing the **asymptotic causal-state manifold** from $\mathbb{R}^d$ onto $E^{u/c}$ ($\dim E^{u/c} = r$):
  - At $d_{\mathrm{mem}} \ge r$, $M_t = \hat{S}_{t|t}^{u/c}$ preserves the asymptotic causal state ($\eta = 1$ on $E^{u/c}$, zero-decay continuity).
  - At $d_{\mathrm{mem}} < r$, $M_t$ is strictly coarser than the asymptotic causal-state partition on $E^{u/c}$, forcing irreducible state uncertainty amplified by $A_{u/c}^\tau$.

## 5. Active Stress-Tests, Counterexamples & Failure Modes (R2)

### 5.1 Stress-Test 1: Jordan Blocks with $|\lambda| = 1$ ($J_k(1)$)
- **Trap**: $J_k(1)$ has topological entropy $H_{\mathrm{top}}(J_k(1)) = 0$ and geometric multiplicity $\dim \ker(J_k(1) - I_k) = 1$, which naively suggests $d_{\mathrm{mem}}^* = 0$ or $1$.
- **Exact Result**: By Theorems 2.2 and 3.1, $d_{\mathrm{mem}}^* = k$ (the **algebraic** multiplicity). Keeping $d_{\mathrm{mem}} = 1$ (whether the eigenvector $e_0$, leaving $\Omega((t+\tau)^{2(k-1)})$ deterministic error, or the top coordinate $e_{k-1}$, leaving $\Theta(\sigma_W^2 t^{2k-1})$ conditional variance on $x_{t,0}$ under persistent noise) fails catastrophically. Only $d_{\mathrm{mem}} \ge k$ stores the full generalized eigenspace and stabilizes error to $0$.

### 5.2 Stress-Test 2: Non-Normal Transient Amplification ($\rho(A) < 1$) & Kreiss Matrix Theorem
- **Trap**: Single-step Lyapunov stability ($\|A\|_2 < 1$) fails on non-normal matrices. Take $A = \rho I_k + \gamma N_k$ with $0 < \rho < 1$ and $\gamma \gg 1$. Since $\rho(A) = \rho < 1$, we have $r = 0$, $d_{\mathrm{mem}}^* = 0$, and $\sup_{t,\tau \ge 0} \mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) < \infty$.
- **Exact Transient Spike**: For $k = 2$, $(A^t v)_0 = \rho^t v_0 + t \rho^{t-1} \gamma v_1$ ($t \ge 1$). For general $k$, $(A^t)_{0, k-1} = \binom{t}{k-1} \rho^{t-k+1} \gamma^{k-1}$; in the slow-decay regime $\ln(1/\rho) \ll k-1$ ($\rho$ near $1$), this peaks near $t^* \approx \frac{k-1}{\ln(1/\rho)}$ at height $\Theta\!\big((\frac{\gamma}{\ln(1/\rho)})^{k-1}\big) \gg 1$ before asymptotically decaying (while for general $\rho \in (0, 1)$, discrete clamping gives $t^* = \max\big(k-1,\, \lfloor \frac{k-1}{\ln(1/\rho)} \rfloor\big)$—or $t^* = 1$ with peak height $\gamma$ when $k = 2$ and $\ln(1/\rho) > 1$).
- **Kreiss Matrix Theorem**: Define the Kreiss constant $\mathcal{K}(A) := \sup_{|z| > 1} (|z| - 1) \|(z I_d - A)^{-1}\|_2$. For any $A \in \mathbb{C}^{d \times d}$ with $\rho(A) \le 1$,
  $$\mathcal{K}(A) \le \sup_{t \ge 0} \|A^t\|_2 \le e d \, \mathcal{K}(A). \tag{5.1}$$
  *(Proof of the lower bound)*: For any $|z| > 1$, the convergent Neumann series $(z I_d - A)^{-1} = \sum_{t=0}^\infty z^{-(t+1)} A^t$ gives $\|(z I_d - A)^{-1}\|_2 \le (\sup_{t \ge 0} \|A^t\|_2) \sum_{t=0}^\infty |z|^{-(t+1)} = \frac{\sup_{t \ge 0} \|A^t\|_2}{|z| - 1}$; taking $\sup_{|z| > 1}$ proves $\mathcal{K}(A) \le \sup_{t \ge 0} \|A^t\|_2$. (The sharp upper bound $e d \mathcal{K}(A)$ is Spijker's theorem, 1991.)
  *Consequence*: In the super-critical regime $d_{\mathrm{mem}} = r$, discarding $E^s$ keeps error finite as $t, \tau \to \infty$, but finite-horizon spikes near $\tau \approx t^*$ scale as $\sup_t \|A_s^t\|_2^2 \ge \mathcal{K}(A_s)^2$ (and up to $\mathcal{K}(A_s)^4$ when combined with steady-state covariance buildup).

### 5.3 Stress-Test 3: Periodic External Log Retrieval (1 Query Every $T$ Steps)

Suppose the online Markov compressor $M_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ (or bit-rate $b$) can additionally issue **1 external query** to the lossless observation log $X_{0:t}$ every $T \ge 1$ steps ($t \in T \mathbb{N}_0$). Does this lower $d_{\mathrm{mem}}^*$?

1. **Late queries ($\tau \to \infty$): $d_{\mathrm{mem}}^* = r$ is INVARIANT (NOT lowered)**:
   At query time $t = mT + s$ ($0 \le s < T$), the agent answers queries for $v^\top S_{t+\tau}$ using only $M_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ (with no external retrieval during the prediction window $(t, t+\tau]$). If $d_{\mathrm{mem}} < r$, then by Theorem 2.1/2.2 at time $t$, any affine/continuous $M_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ fails to span $E^{u/c}$, leaving a residual variance $\ge c_{\mathrm{local}} > 0$ (from noise $W_{t-1}$ just prior to $t$, or uncertainty accrued over $s \ge 1$ steps since the last retrieval). Propagating forward to $t + \tau$ forces $\sup_{v \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}} \mathcal{E}_{t,\tau}(v) = \Omega(|\lambda_{\min}(A_u)|^{2\tau})$ (or $\Omega(\tau^{2(k-1)})$ for $E^c$) regardless of $T$. **Periodic external retrieval does NOT lower $d_{\mathrm{mem}}^* = r$ for late queries.**
2. **Present-time ($\tau = 0$) unbounded full-state reset every $T$ steps**:
   If at $t = mT$ the external retrieval injects the exact $d$-dimensional Kalman state $\hat{S}_{mT|mT} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ (violating the $d_{\mathrm{mem}} < r$ capacity constraint at the reset steps via $\Omega(d)$ continuous coordinates), then at $\tau = 0$ the error resets to $0$ at $t = mT$ and diverges only across the inter-retrieval window $s \in \{1, \dots, T-1\}$, replacing $t$-divergence with a **$T$-dependent prefactor**:
   $$\sup_{t \ge 0} \sup_{v \in \mathbb{S}^{d-1}} \mathcal{E}_{t,0}(v) = \Theta\!\left(|\lambda_{\max}(A_u)|^{2T}\right) \quad \text{(or } \Theta(T^{2k-1}) \text{ for } J_k(1)\text{)}.$$
3. **Rate-limited external retrieval ($B_{\mathrm{ext}}$ bits every $T$ steps)**:
   If each external query returns $B_{\mathrm{ext}} < \infty$ bits alongside online rate $b$ bits/step, the amortized bit-rate over $mT$ steps is $b_{\mathrm{eff}} := b + \frac{B_{\mathrm{ext}}}{T}$. Applying the entropy-growth and rate-reduction steps of Theorem 2.3 over each $T$-step block gives
   $$h(S_{mT}^u \mid M_{mT}) \ge h(S_0^u \mid M_0) + m T \left(H_{\mathrm{top}}(A) - b - \frac{B_{\mathrm{ext}}}{T}\right).$$
   Thus: (i) if $b + B_{\mathrm{ext}}/T < H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$, exponential divergence in $t$ persists unabated at rate $H_{\mathrm{top}}(A) - b_{\mathrm{eff}}$; (ii) fixed $B_{\mathrm{ext}} = O(1)$ only shifts the bit-rate threshold by $O(1/T) \to 0$ as $T \to \infty$; and (iii) in the band $b < H_{\mathrm{top}}(A) \le b + B_{\mathrm{ext}}/T$, the inter-retrieval error still inflates as $\Omega(|\lambda|^{2T})$ between queries.

### 5.4 Nonlinear / Hybrid and Non-Gaussian / Heavy-Tailed Edge Cases

- **Non-Gaussian finite-variance noise ($\Sigma_W \prec \infty$)**: Theorems 2.1, 2.2, 2.4, 3.1, and 3.2 use only second moments / independence and remain valid verbatim (with $\hat{S}_{t|t}$ interpreted as conditional expectation, or linear MMSE if restricted to linear estimators). Theorem 2.3 remains valid whenever $h(S_0^u) > -\infty$ because $\mathcal{N}(0, \tilde{\Sigma}_t)$ maximizes differential entropy among *all* distributions with second-moment matrix $\tilde{\Sigma}_t$.
- **Heavy-tailed infinite-variance noise ($\alpha$-stable, $\alpha < 2$)**: MSE is $+\infty$ even at $d_{\mathrm{mem}} = d$; however, the differential-entropy growth bound (2.5) and $L^p$ ($p < \alpha$) or quantile-width divergence continue to hold whenever $h(S_0^u) > -\infty$.
- **Smooth nonlinear dynamics $S_{t+1} = F(S_t) + W_t$**: Locally near an invariant hyperbolic/center manifold, $H_{\mathrm{top}}(A)$ generalizes to the Pesin sum of positive Lyapunov exponents $\sum_{\chi_i > 0} \chi_i$ and $r$ counts non-negative Lyapunov exponents ($\chi_i \ge 0$), though global multistability or foldings can require extra discrete topological state labels beyond the local tangent dimension.
