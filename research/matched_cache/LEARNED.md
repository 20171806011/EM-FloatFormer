# Learned per-window cache

The inference API is `model.encode(windows)`, with three complex samples per window. Every output is an integer in `range(2**(B//2))` for total two-window budget $B=4$ or $8$. The two windows share weights. No pilot, label, mother ID, question or rotation ID is an encoder input. The static posterior table and network weights are common model parameters. The encoder discards its raw observations and temporary floating-point activations after emitting the indices.

## Training

The distillation targets are four posterior probabilities for the received symbol orientation under the single-window synthetic model. They are computed on the training windows using the pilot-free physical likelihood. The model therefore receives analytic supervision about the observation distribution. At inference it must obtain its code directly from the six real I/Q coordinates. The six coordinates go through a 16-unit tanh layer and a four-class softmax, with 180 trainable scalars including biases. We minimize soft-target cross-entropy with Adam, step size 0.005, moment coefficients 0.9 and 0.999, epsilon $10^{-8}$, 48 epochs, shuffled batches of 256. NumPy PCG64 with the run seed controls both network initialization and shuffling. No stopping or checkpoint choice uses validation or test loss.

We next quantize the network predictions by minimizing forward KL distortion to a small set of posterior vectors. Initialization chooses one training prediction uniformly and each further center proportionally to the nearest-center KL distortion. Lloyd fitting alternates nearest-center assignment and arithmetic mean updates for up to 30 iterations. For the fixed assignments, the decoded posterior is the mean of the analytical training targets in that cell; this is the cross-entropy optimal readout on those examples. Encoder centers and decoder posterior prototypes are therefore saved separately. Each model requires all its canonical centers to have training support. Static weights, centers and tables can be saved with `model.save(path)` and loaded with `learned.load(path)`. The archive contains only numeric arrays and loads with pickling disabled.

## Rotations and what can be attributed to training

**Unconstrained model.** All six coordinates enter the network in the receiver coordinates. The assignment can select any of four or sixteen centroids. A noisy input and its rotated partner can map to different, inconsistently related states even if both decisions are correct. The supervised loss encourages approximating equivariant teacher probabilities, but neither the unconstrained network nor the codebook enforces this relation.

**Equivariant model.** Each input window is rotated into a canonical quadrant, determined by the sum of its samples, with sectors half-open and centered on the four constellation points. The orientation $r$ is inferred from the window itself. Training rotates the associated target by $-r$ before fitting the network and codebook. With $k$ canonical centers, inference emits the single integer $rk+c$, where $c$ is the learned cell. The decoded posterior for this state is the canonical posterior rolled forward by $r$ labels. At 4 total bits there is just one canonical cell: all encoding decisions are determined by the hard quadrant. Training can calibrate the readout but cannot change its partition. At 8 total bits four canonical cells share each orientation, and their assignments depend on the trained network.

Multiplying the entire window by $i$ increments $r$ and leaves the canonical I/Q values identical. Thus its decoded four-probability vector rotates one position regardless of the fitted weights. If the sum vanishes, we use its first nonzero sample as the orientation anchor; the all-zero window is assigned quadrant zero and has probability zero under this noise model. The model does not derive exact rotation symmetry from data. The shared evidence readout, outside the encoder, identifies duplicates by the supplied observation ID. Duplicate invariance measures the behavior of that readout under the disclosed reference protocol.
