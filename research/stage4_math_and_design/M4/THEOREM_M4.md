### 1.1 The three representations

For R1, \(T=q^*K^{-1}(R-d)/(\widehat a q^*K^{-1}q)\) is the noise-whitened matched estimate. This follows by expanding the Gaussian likelihood: the only class-dependent observation term is \(2\operatorname{Re}(a^*\bar S q^*K^{-1}(R-d))\). The true variance of \(T\) is \((\widehat a^2 q^*K^{-1}q)^{-1}\); a fixed unitary on the whitened taps can align the steering direction with \(e\). Replacing \(q\) by \(\widehat q\) leaves a component \(\mu_\perp(S)\) orthogonal to the retained direction. With exactly whitened unit covariance and known Gaussian steering,

\[
0\le I(S;R)-I(S;T)=I(S;R_\perp\mid T)\le\min\{\log4,\ \mathbb E\|\mu_\perp(S)\|^2\}.
\]

The bound follows by comparing the conditional off-axis distribution to independent standard noise, using the KL chain rule. Its upper term vanishes for correct steering. Pulse-shape error, signal-dependent ISI, and improper noise require a fuller law. One may whiten an augmented real vector when the pseudo-covariance is nonzero, using the real Gaussian formula with its factor of \(1/2\).

The optional forced-calibration control is \(T'=\alpha T+\sqrt{V_0-\alpha^2v}\,Z\), where \(Z\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)\), \(\alpha=\min(1,\sqrt{V_0/v})\), and \(v\) is a causal estimate of post-AGC thermal variance. With correct \(v\), this matches the thermal variance, and its conditional Gaussian KL becomes \(|\alpha\mu_S-S|^2/V_0\). When \(v>V_0\), attenuation alone is invertible at continuous input and shrinks the signal mean. When \(v<V_0\), adding noise degrades information. Real noise with random scale, coherent sidebands or ISI retains those effects through this operation. Use the SNR-preserving R3 in the primary comparison, with causal variance and gain-uncertainty summaries available equally to all matched-information decision rules. ### 1.2 Conditional KL and perturbation terms

For a nuisance state \(U\) and class \(s\), take the \(m\)-complex observation law \(P_{s,U}=\mathcal{CN}(\mu_{s,U},K_U)\) with nonsingular \(K_U\) and compare to \(Q_s=\mathcal{CN}(\mu_{0,s},K_0)\). Expanding the log densities and averaging the quadratic terms yields

\[
D(P_{s,U}\|Q_s)=\log\frac{\det K_0}{\det K_U}-m+operatorname{tr}(K_0^{-1}K_U)
+(\mu_{s,U}-\mu_{0,s})^*K_0^{-1}(\mu_{s,U}-\mu_{0,s}). \tag{1}
\]

For a scalar in canonical constellation coordinates define \(g=\widehat a/a=1+\delta>0\), \(a=|h|\), \(L=h_{12}+\ell\) in symbols starting at the chosen phase reference, \(\vartheta=\epsilon_\omega L\), \(b=\Delta d/a\), and \(\psi_L=\psi_0+2\pi\Delta f L/f_{\rm sym}\). Include a residual relative complex spur \(r\) as a multiplicative sideband and a fixed conditional neighboring-symbol ISI term \(j_s\) in gain-normalized units. Its known-phase, known-variance law has

\[
T\mid s,U\sim\mathcal{CN}\!\left(\frac{s(e^{i\vartheta}+re^{i\psi_L})+b+j_s}{g},\frac{v_{\rm phys}}{g^2}\right).
\]

Thus, with \(\lambda=v_{\rm phys}/(g^2 V_0)\), the exact KL to the nominal scalar is

\[
\lambda-1-\log\lambda +\frac{|s(e^{i\vartheta}-g+re^{i\psi_L})+b+j_s|^2}{g^2 V_0}. \tag{2}
\]

This is conditional on \(U\). A term may be stochastic or correlated with others. Keep the coherent cross terms, or use the triangle inequality to upper bound the squared numerator by \((|\delta|+2|\sin(\vartheta/2)|+r+|b|+|j_s|)^2\). With uniform \(s\), constant \(b\), and \(j_s=0\), the exact average squared shift is \(|e^{i\vartheta}-g+re^{i\psi_L}|^2+|b|^2\). At gain and variance match, isolated \(b=0.3\) costs `0.142640` nats, isolated \(r=0.26\) costs `0.107139`, and isolated CFO costs \(4\sin^2(\epsilon_\omega L/2)/V_0\). At `0.020 rad/symbol`, lags 4, 25 and 126 cost `0.010138`, `0.388037` and `5.746671` nats. A centered independent Gaussian residual CFO with standard deviation \(\sigma_\omega\) gives average phase energy \(2[1-\exp(-L^2\sigma_\omega^2/2)]\). This follows from the characteristic function of the residual. It bounds KL to the fixed phase likelihood after dividing by \(V_0\). The bound does not grow unboundedly with lag. A coherent spur of 0.26 amplitude has power ratio 0.0676 and phase beat about `0.018887 rad/symbol` at a 1503 Hz offset and 500 ksym/s. Represent its phase jointly across windows. The measured sideband ratio alone does not distinguish a multiplicative sideband from an additive tone. For an additive tone, move \(re^{i\psi_L}\) into \(b\) within (2).

Pluto normalized residual DC can be much larger at low gain for the same absolute subtraction error. For \(\widehat a=a(1+\delta)\), the DC-only term is \(|\Delta d|^2/(\widehat a^2 V_0)\). Its AC-coupling component can depend on the transmitted sequence, which may create conditional mean and covariance terms together. For a proper Gaussian AR(1) approximation to the **total** tap residual with normalized covariance \(K=\lambda V_0 R_\rho\) and \((R_\rho)_{ij}=\rho^{i-j}\) for \(i\ge j\) and Hermitian symmetry, Eq. (1) gives covariance cost

\[
m(\lambda-1-\log\lambda)-(m-1)\log(1-|\rho|^2),\qquad |\rho|<1. \tag{3}
\]

The determinant follows by factoring each successive innovation with variance \(1-|\rho|^2\). If ISI contributes only a portion of colored variance, use \(K=(v_{\rm AWGN}I+v_{\rm ISI}R_\rho)/g^2\) in (1). A single measured correlation coefficient without a full joint law or eigenvalue floor cannot determine KL. Discrete neighboring symbols yield a finite mixture if those symbols are marginalized. Overlapping symbol windows and common random gain or CFO can induce extra dependence even with white thermal noise. Stack the full covariance or use the joint likelihood; summing isolated-window KL is exact only under the appropriate factorization.

For a Gaussian nuisance mixture with the same class prior, including a class-dependent nuisance law if needed, the exact identity is

\[
D(P_{S,Y}\|Q_{S,Y})=\mathbb E_{S,U}D(P_{Y\mid S,U}\|Q_{Y\mid S})-I_P(U;Y\mid S). \tag{4}
\]

This follows by attaching the same \(P(U\mid S)\) to the nominal joint and expanding KL in two orders. Use the resulting upper bound if the expectation is finite. For divergent terms apply the chain rule as a sum of nonnegative quantities. The fixed-model Bayes posterior regret is

\[
\mathbb E_Y D(P_{S\mid Y}\|Q_{S\mid Y})=D(P_{S,Y}\|Q_{S,Y})-D(P_Y\|Q_Y). \tag{5}
\]

If the comparator is the frozen `s17` table \(q_{17}\), the exact excess vs full observation Bayes is \(I_P(S;Y\mid Z)+\mathbb E_Z D(P_{S\mid Z}\|q_{17}(\cdot\mid Z))\), with \(Z=\mathrm{s17}(Y)\). The second term contains train-domain calibration and approximation error. Eq. (5) cannot bound it by zero when the input law matches training. For a positive frozen table, its absolute expected NLL change between two laws is at most \(-\log(\min q_{17})\,t\), where \(t\) is total variation after the common mapping and \(t\le\min(1,\sqrt{D/2})\) for a finite joint KL bound \(D\). MAP SER change for a fixed rule is at most \(t\). If the priors on \(S\) coincide, \(|I_P(S;Y)-I_Q(S;Y)|\le\min(\log4, h_2(t)+t\log4)\). Decompose each law into its common mass and its residual of mass \(t\); the conditional entropy of the binary mixture indicator is at most \(h_2(t)\). Apply the same argument to codes by data processing. An unbounded bound in (2) or (4) is compatible with finite code MI and bounded table NLL.

### 1.3 Student scale, Rayleigh scale, and the frozen nonlinearity

Let \(W\sim\mathcal{CN}_m(0,\Sigma)\) and \(G>0\) be independent given the true channel and authorized history, with scale estimate \(\widehat a=aG\). For \(X=W/G\) in gain-normalized noise units the exact density is

\[
p_X(x)=\frac{\mathbb E[G^{2m}e^{-G^2x^*\Sigma^{-1}x}]}{\pi^m\det\Sigma}. \tag{6}
\]

If \(G^2\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(\nu,\text{rate}=\nu)\), the integral gives the proper complex Student density about zero

\[
p_X(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\nu+m)}{\Gamma(\nu)(\pi\nu)^m\det\Sigma}
\left(1+\frac{x^*\Sigma^{-1}x}{\nu}\right)^{-(\nu+m)}.
\]

In the corresponding whitened real coordinates this is a Student distribution with \(2\nu\) degrees of freedom. With \(A=\Sigma_0^{-1}\Sigma\) and \(\nu>1\) the centered KL to the proper Gaussian \(\mathcal{CN}(0,\Sigma_0)\) is exactly

\[
\log\frac{\Gamma(\nu+m)}{\Gamma(\nu)\nu^m}+\log\frac{\det\Sigma_0}{\det\Sigma}
-(\nu+m)[\psi(\nu+m)-\psi(\nu)]+\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}\operatorname{tr}A. \tag{7}
\]

Indeed \(x^*\Sigma^{-1}x/\nu\sim\mathrm{BetaPrime}(m,\nu)\), since it is the ratio of unit-rate Gamma variables with shapes \(m,\nu\). Its log moment is the digamma difference and its first moment is \(m/(\nu-1)\). A fixed location shift adds the squared \(\Sigma_0^{-1}\)-norm of the shift. Moment matching uses \(\Sigma_\star=\nu\Sigma/(\nu-1)\). The excess over the covariance-only Gaussian mismatch is exactly \(D(P_X\|\mathcal{CN}(0,\Sigma_\star))\), by matching expectations of both Gaussian log densities. For scalar \(\Sigma_0=\Sigma\), (7) reduces to \(1/[\nu(\nu-1)]\). At \(\nu=3\) this is `0.166667` nats, of which `0.072132` nats remain after moment matching. If only \(T\) has Student noise and the lift ancillary is Gaussian, use \(m=1\).

Rayleigh denominator with \(\mathbb E G^2=1\) is \(\nu=1\). In one complex dimension \(P(|X|^2/\Sigma>q)=(1+q)^{-1}\), so \(\mathbb E|X|^2=\infty\), and forward KL to any nondegenerate Gaussian is infinite. The normalized Student entropy is finite here. More generally an infinite second moment gives infinite KL to a Gaussian by testing bounded truncations of a sufficiently small positive multiple of \(\|X\|^2\) in the variational KL lower bound. If the amplitude estimator itself has a folded Student law, substitute its density \(2f_{t_d}(g/b)/b\) for \(g>0\) in (6), where \(f_{t_d}\) is the standard real Student density and \(b\) its scale. That exact integral is a different scale mixture, with infinite second moment since the folded density is positive at zero. A noisy complex pilot LS amplitude \(|h+E|/a\) with nondegenerate proper Gaussian \(E\) is conditionally Rician. Its radial density has positive linear coefficient at zero, so \(\mathbb E G^{-2}=\infty\) even for nonzero \(h\). If `a` itself is Rayleigh but estimated exactly and both amplitude and noise scale with it, \(G=1\) and the heavy tail argument does not apply. If a new thermal sample and the scale estimator share noise, the exact law uses \(W\mid G\) in (6) and cannot be inferred from the gain histogram alone.

AGC usually divides the signal too. For \(T=(\mu_s+W)/G\), the exact class-conditional density replaces \(G^2x^*\Sigma^{-1}x\) in (6) with \((Gx-\mu_s)^*\Sigma^{-1}(Gx-\mu_s)\). It is a noncentral scale mixture and generally has no centered Student formula. Absolute Rayleigh fading and fixed receiver noise can also produce a heavy-tailed unconditional noise term even with exact gain estimation, since averaging \(W/a\) over \(a\) invokes the Rayleigh case. A causal guard \(\widehat a\leftarrow\max(\widehat a,a_{\min})\), with floor fixed from training/calibration and carried in the uncertainty flags, makes \(\mathbb E G^{-2}\) finite conditional on \(a\). Fit variance floors, clip estimator outliers as registered on dev, and model or report flags. When estimating a noise scale with \(\nu\) complex residual degrees of freedom independent of the new observation and a correctly specified proper Gaussian linear fit, the normalized residual power estimate has Gamma shape \(\nu\). In a rank-\(p\) complex fit to \(n\) such pilots, \(\nu=n-p\) follows from orthogonal residual projection. Estimated CFO, branch selection, regularization, symbol decisions, correlated pilots, and unknown DC break this pivot unless accounted for. Use a robust likelihood or (4) with measured nuisance uncertainty in those cases.

For a finite label alphabet, the unrestricted scalar posterior for any specified mixture is obtained by normalizing those four densities. Its uniform-input MI is exactly

\[
I(S;T)=\log4+\frac14\sum_s\int p_s(t)\log\frac{p_s(t)}{\sum_v p_v(t)}\,dt.
\]

If \(U\perp S\), the loss of hiding \(U\) is exactly \(I(S;U\mid T)\). Given a fixed coherent gain, phase and bias, invertible scaling or rotation preserves information up to the effective SNR; a nominal decoder may nonetheless incur the posterior regret in (5). A uniform hidden quarter-turn makes the four single-symbol densities identical, so MI is zero even at infinite physical SNR. Relative-symbol and retransmission questions can still have information through shared latent phase. For fixed circular symmetric noise with an equivariant QPSK model, the uniform prior achieves the constrained capacity by averaging the four rotated input priors. With other empirical code channels, the verifier computes a Blahut–Arimoto lower and upper bound for the fitted transition matrix. These brackets measure optimization error on the Monte Carlo counts and carry sampling uncertainty.

For `s17`, along a fixed nonzero canonical ray \(x=cu\), \(c>0\), the sum sector is scale invariant. At low gain \(\tanh(cuW_1+b_1)\to\tanh b_1\), so the local code becomes constant if the winning cross-entropy score has a positive margin. The hidden deviation is at most \(c\|uW_1\|\). Converting it through \(W_2\) and the \(1/2\)-Lipschitz softmax, the bound on pairwise score changes is \((c/2)\|uW_1\|\|W_2\|_{2\to2}\|\log C_i-\log C_j\|\) for centers \(C_i,C_j\). A strict margin greater than this perturbation proves stability. For high gain, neuron \(j\) with \(d_j=(uW_1)_j\ne0\) approaches \(\operatorname{sgn}(d_j)\) with error at most \(2e^{-2(c|d_j|-|b_{1j}|)}\) once \(c|d_j|>|b_{1j}|\); neurons with zero projection stay at \(\tanh b_{1j}\). Apply the same score-margin test to the limiting logits and center choices. This proves radial saturation on each interior ray with strict margin. The four turns can remain informative. At exact all-zero input the encoder chooses its defined tie behavior. `verify_m4.py` finds `[1,5,9,13]` in the low-gain limit and `[0,4,8,12]` on its high-gain ray. Ties, near-orthogonal directions, and random scales near zero preclude a global AGC threshold.
